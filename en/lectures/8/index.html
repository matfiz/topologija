Lecture 8

Tangent spaces, Lie groups and homogeneous spaces

What this lecture does

A tangent vector records the initial velocity of a curve without choosing an ambient Euclidean space. Coordinate changes relate its components by their Jacobian matrices. We use these vectors to differentiate maps and describe the infinitesimal directions of matrix groups. A transitive smooth action of a Lie group presents a manifold as a quotient by the subgroup fixing a point. The sphere and the qubit state space give two concrete quotient descriptions.

8.3 The tangent bundle

The tangent bundle is the disjoint union of all tangent spaces: TM={(p,v):p∈M, v∈TpM}.\tangentbundle M=\{(p,v):p\in M,\ v\in T_pM\}.TM={(p,v):p∈M, v∈Tp​M}. Its fibre over ppp is the inverse image πTM−1(p)\pi_{TM}^{-1}(p)πTM−1​(p), identified with TpMT_pMTp​M. Vectors at different base points belong to different fibres. They cannot be added without a further identification.

Proposition 8.3 (Manifold structure on the tangent bundlesketched). If MMM is a smooth nnn-manifold, then TM\tangentbundle MTM has a natural smooth structure of dimension 2n2n2n. The projection is a smooth submersion.

We sketch its chart construction. A chart xxx on U⊆MU\subseteq MU⊆M gives coordinates (x(p),vx)(x(p),v_x)(x(p),vx​) on πTM−1(U)\pi_{TM}^{-1}(U)πTM−1​(U) with image x(U)×Rnx(U)\times\R^nx(U)×Rn. A countable atlas downstairs gives a countable basis in these product coordinates. This gives Hausdorffness and second countability. 8.4 Lie groups and tangent matrices

A Lie group is a smooth manifold G\LiegroupG that is also a group, with smooth multiplication G×G→G\Liegroup\times\Liegroup\to\LiegroupG×G→G and smooth inversion G→G\Liegroup\to\LiegroupG→G. Write e\groupidentitye for its identity. For K∈{R,C}\mathbb K\in\{\R,\C\}K∈{R,C}, the general linear group is GL(n,K)={A∈Kn×n:det⁡A≠0}.\GL(n,\mathbb K)=\{A\in\mathbb K^{n\times n}:\det A\ne0\}.GL(n,K)={A∈Kn×n:detA=0}. The special orthogonal group is SO(n)={A∈O(n):det⁡A=1}.\SO(n)=\{A\in\Orth(n):\det A=1\}.SO(n)={A∈O(n):detA=1}. A matrix subgroup is a subgroup of a general linear group. For complex matrices, the ambient space is regarded as a real manifold.

Proposition 8.4 (Regular matrix groupsproved given the regular level set theorem). A matrix subgroup that is a regular level set has the induced structure of a Lie group. The tangent space TISO(3)T_I\SO(3)TI​SO(3) is the vector space so(3)={A∈R3×3:AT=−A}\so(3)=\{A\in\R^{3\times3}:A^T=-A\}so(3)={A∈R3×3:AT=−A}.

Proof. The regular level set theorem gives an embedded manifold. Matrix multiplication is polynomial in real entries. Matrix inversion is smooth on invertible matrices, by the adjugate formula divided by the nonzero determinant. These maps preserve the subgroup. In adapted submanifold charts, their restrictions retain the first manifold-coordinate components of their ambient expressions, so the restrictions are smooth. They are the group’s multiplication and inversion, as required.

The orthogonal constraint and its surjective derivative were computed in Lecture 7. The determinant-one part is open in O(3)\Orth(3)O(3), since its only determinants are +1+1+1 and −1-1−1. It therefore has the same tangent space at III. By Proposition 8.2, that space is the kernel of A↦AT+AA\mapsto A^T+AA↦AT+A. This is precisely the antisymmetric matrices.

8.5 Actions and homogeneous spaces

A smooth left action of a Lie group on MMM is a smooth map G×M→M\Liegroup\times M\to MG×M→M, written (g,p)↦g⋅p(g,p)\mapsto g\cdot p(g,p)↦g⋅p, satisfying e⋅p=p\groupidentity\cdot p=pe⋅p=p and (gh)⋅p=g⋅(h⋅p)(gh)\cdot p=g\cdot(h\cdot p)(gh)⋅p=g⋅(h⋅p). The orbit of ppp is {g⋅p:g∈G}\{g\cdot p:g\in\Liegroup\}{g⋅p:g∈G}. The orbit map at ppp is the map G→M\Liegroup\to MG→M given by g↦g⋅pg\mapsto g\cdot pg↦g⋅p. The action is transitive when this orbit is all of MMM. A manifold with a transitive smooth action is a homogeneous space. The stabiliser (or isotropy subgroup) of ppp is H={g∈G:g⋅p=p}.H=\{g\in\Liegroup:g\cdot p=p\}.H={g∈G:g⋅p=p}. It is a subgroup by the action laws. It is closed, being the inverse image of the closed singleton {p}\{p\}{p} under the continuous orbit map.

For a subgroup HHH, the left coset gHgHgH is {gh:h∈H}\{gh:h\in H\}{gh:h∈H}. The set of left cosets, with the quotient topology, is written G/H\Liegroup/HG/H. Two elements have the same coset exactly when g1−1g2∈Hg_1^{-1}g_2\in Hg1−1​g2​∈H. An embedded Lie subgroup is a subgroup with an embedded smooth manifold structure and the restricted smooth group operations.

Theorem 8.5 (Closed subgroups and homogeneous spacescited). A closed subgroup HHH of a Lie group is an embedded Lie subgroup. The quotient G/H\Liegroup/HG/H has a smooth manifold structure of dimension dim⁡G−dim⁡H\dim\Liegroup-\dim HdimG−dimH for which the quotient map is a smooth submersion. For a transitive smooth action on MMM with stabiliser HHH at ppp, the map gH↦g⋅pgH\mapsto g\cdot pgH↦g⋅p is a diffeomorphism G/H→M\Liegroup/H\to MG/H→M.

These are Lee, second edition, Theorems 20.12, 21.17 and 21.18. The action identifies cosets with points: if g1⋅p=g2⋅pg_1\cdot p=g_2\cdot pg1​⋅p=g2​⋅p, then g1−1g2∈Hg_1^{-1}g_2\in Hg1−1​g2​∈H, and the converse follows from the action law. Closedness cannot be omitted. The irrational line of Lecture 7 is a subgroup HHH of T2T^2T2, since adding its parameters multiplies its circle components. It is dense. Indeed, arbitrarily small nonzero fractional parts of integer multiples of α\alphaα exist by the pigeonhole argument there, allowing either sign of the integer. The subgroup is proper: over first coordinate 111, only the countably many points with second coordinate e2πiαme^{2\pi\ii\alpha m}e2πiαm, m∈Zm\in\Zm∈Z, occur. Hence HHH is not closed. If T2/HT^2/HT2/H were Hausdorff, its singleton identity coset would be closed. Its inverse image HHH would then be closed, a contradiction. This quotient is not a manifold of our definition.

8.6 The sphere and the Hopf map

The standard action of SO(n+1)\SO(n+1)SO(n+1) on Sn\sphere nSn is transitive. Given a unit vector, extend it to an oriented orthonormal basis by Gram–Schmidt, recalled from Linear Algebra for Physics. Use it as the last column of an orthogonal matrix, completing the other columns with positive orientation. That matrix sends the last coordinate unit vector to the chosen point. The stabiliser consists of matrices with a block in SO(n)\SO(n)SO(n) on its orthogonal complement and a last diagonal entry 111. The group O(n+1)\Orth(n+1)O(n+1) is a regular level set by Lecture 7, so it is a Lie group by Proposition 8.4. Its determinant-one part is open and hence is also a Lie group. The sphere action is the restriction of the polynomial map (g,p)↦gp(g,p)\mapsto gp(g,p)↦gp, so it is smooth. Applying the cited theorem gives Sn≅SO(n+1)/SO(n)(n≥1).\sphere n\cong\SO(n+1)/\SO(n)\qquad(n\ge1).Sn≅SO(n+1)/SO(n)(n≥1). The identification is a diffeomorphism. In particular, the orbit map g↦ge3g\mapsto g e_3g↦ge3​ exhibits S2=SO(3)/SO(2)\sphere2=\SO(3)/\SO(2)S2=SO(3)/SO(2). The subgroup SO(2)\SO(2)SO(2) rotates the other two columns while leaving the last one fixed. These quotient descriptions concern smooth manifolds; the stabiliser is not a normal subgroup in general, so the quotient need not be a group.

Summary

Tangent vectors are velocity classes. A chart identifies them with coordinate columns, and its Jacobian gives their transformation law. Differentials send curve velocities to image velocities and satisfy the chain rule. The tangent space of a regular level set is the kernel of the constraint’s derivative. Closed stabilisers give smooth quotient manifolds by cited theorems. The rotation and qubit actions identify the sphere with SO(3)/SO(2)\SO(3)/\SO(2)SO(3)/SO(2) and SU(2)/U(1)\SU(2)/\U(1)SU(2)/U(1) respectively. Lecture 9 studies curves whose velocities are prescribed at every point by a vector field.
