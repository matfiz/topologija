Lecture 12

Orientation, integration and Stokes’ theorem

An orientation specifies the sign of a top-degree form. It lets us integrate forms on a manifold. Stokes’ theorem identifies the integral of an exterior derivative with the integral on the boundary, using outward-first orientation. We prove it in every dimension and apply it to the flux of the Coulomb form.

12.1 Orientations and oriented charts

For an nnn-dimensional real vector space with n≥1n\ge1n≥1, two ordered bases have the same orientation if their change-of-basis determinant is positive. There are two classes. A frame on an open set is an ordered list of smooth tangent fields that is a basis at every point. An orientation of a manifold chooses a class in each tangent space, locally represented by a smooth frame. A manifold is orientable if it admits such a choice, and oriented when a choice has been made.

An oriented atlas has positive Jacobian determinants on all chart transitions. Its coordinate frames give compatible orientation classes: the change of frame uses the inverse Jacobian, whose determinant is also positive. Conversely, near any point an orientation is represented by a frame. Its determinant relative to a coordinate frame is continuous and nonzero. Shrink to a connected chart so that the sign is constant. If necessary replace x1x^1x1 by −x1-x^1−x1. The coordinate frame then represents the chosen orientation. Charts obtained this way have positive transitions. This proves the equivalence of orientations and oriented atlases.

A nowhere-zero top form μ\volumeformμ selects the bases on which its value is positive. These choices are locally represented by coordinate frames, because its coefficient in a connected chart has constant sign. For oriented manifolds, a diffeomorphism is orientation-preserving when its differential sends positive bases to positive bases. In positive charts this means its Jacobian determinant is positive.

For example, on Sn⊂Rn+1\sphere n\subset\R^{n+1}Sn⊂Rn+1 the form μp(v1,…,vn)=det⁡[p v1 ⋯ vn](vj∈TpSn)\volumeform_p(v_1,\ldots,v_n)=\det[p\ v_1\ \cdots\ v_n] \qquad(v_j\in T_p\sphere n)μp​(v1​,…,vn​)=det[p v1​ ⋯ vn​](vj​∈Tp​Sn) is smooth and nowhere zero: the radial vector ppp is independent of the nnn tangent basis vectors. It defines the outward orientation. For n=2n=2n=2 it is the sphere area form of Lecture 10. Lecture 10 computed the north-pole stereographic inverse pullback as −4du∧dv/(1+u2+v2)2-4\dd u\wedge\dd v/(1+u^2+v^2)^2−4du∧dv/(1+u2+v2)2. This chart reverses the outward orientation.

In dimension zero an orientation is a choice of sign +1+1+1 or −1-1−1 at each point. Integration of a function then means its signed sum. This convention includes the endpoints of an oriented interval.

12.2 Partitions of unity and orientability

A collection of sets is locally finite if every point has a neighbourhood meeting only finitely many of them. A smooth partition of unity subordinate to an open cover {Ui}\{U_i\}{Ui​} consists of nonnegative smooth functions ρi\rho_iρi​ such that ∑iρi=1,supp⁡ρi⊂Ui,{supp⁡ρi} is locally finite.\sum_i\rho_i=1,\qquad \supp\rho_i\subset U_i, \qquad\{\supp\rho_i\}\text{ is locally finite}.i∑​ρi​=1,suppρi​⊂Ui​,{suppρi​} is locally finite. It also implies that a compact set meets only finitely many supports: choose finitely many such neighbourhoods covering the compact set.

Choose a smooth bump on each ball that is positive on its smaller ball and supported in the larger one. The coordinate construction uses the smooth function e−1/te^{-1/t}e−1/t for t>0t>0t>0, extended by zero for t≤0t\le0t≤0. In dimension zero the constant function 111 is already a nowhere-zero 0-form, and every discrete manifold has an atlas.

12.3 Integrating a top form

Recall the ordinary change-of-variables theorem: under a smooth diffeomorphism y=f(x)y=f(x)y=f(x), integration of a compactly supported function uses the factor ∣det⁡Df∣|\det Df|∣detDf∣. In positive coordinates the determinant itself is positive. This is the calculus input to integration of forms.

For n≥1n\ge1n≥1, let ω\formω be a smooth compactly supported nnn-form on an oriented nnn-manifold. On a positive chart x ⁣:U→x(U)x\colon U\to x(U)x:U→x(U) write (x−1)∗ω=fdx1∧⋯∧dxn(x^{-1})^*\form=f\dd x^1\wedge\cdots\wedge\dd x^n(x−1)∗ω=fdx1∧⋯∧dxn. If its support lies in UUU, define its integral to be the ordinary integral of fff on x(U)x(U)x(U). For a general support, choose a positive atlas and a subordinate partition and set ∫Mω=∑i∫Uiρiω.\int_M\form=\sum_i\int_{U_i}\rho_i\form.∫M​ω=i∑​∫Ui​​ρi​ω. Only finitely many terms meet the compact support. Each has compact support inside its chart, so every term is defined.

Proposition 12.3 (Independence of choicesproved given partitions of unity and change of variables). The integral is independent of the positive atlas and subordinate partition. Reversing the orientation reverses its sign.

Proof. On overlapping positive charts, the determinant rule for top-form pullback and ordinary change of variables give the same chart integral. For two partitions ρi\rho_iρi​ and χj\chi_jχj​ subordinate to positive charts, insert ∑jχj=1\sum_j\chi_j=1∑j​χj​=1 into each integral for the first partition. The result is the finite double sum of the integrals of ρiχjω\rho_i\chi_j\formρi​χj​ω. Each term is supported in the chart overlap and has the same value in either chart system. Interchanging the sums and using ∑iρi=1\sum_i\rho_i=1∑i​ρi​=1 gives the second partition’s answer. This proves independence. For the opposite orientation, reflect one coordinate. Its derivative has determinant −1-1−1, whereas the absolute Jacobian in ordinary integration is 111. The top-form coefficient changes sign, proving the last assertion. 

For computations, let D⊂RnD\subset\R^nD⊂Rn be a compact parameter region, and let F ⁣:D→MF\colon D\to MF:D→M be smooth with F(D)=MF(D)=MF(D)=M. Here smoothness on DDD means local smooth extension to open neighbourhoods in Rn\R^nRn. Suppose FFF restricts to an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism int⁡D→M∖E\operatorname{int}D\to M\setminus EintD→M∖E, where E=F(∂D)E=F(\boundary D)E=F(∂D). Then integration is computed by pullback: ∫Mω=∫DF∗ω.\int_M\form=\int_D F^*\form.∫M​ω=∫D​F∗ω. Indeed, insert a subordinate partition ρi\rho_iρi​ on MMM. Summing the finitely many nonzero chart integrals proves the formula. These are the hypotheses behind the angular rectangle used below; see also Tu, §23.4, Definition 23.10.

A density has local expressions f∣dx1⋯dxn∣f|\dd x^1\cdots\dd x^n|f∣dx1⋯dxn∣ that transform with the absolute Jacobian. Densities can be integrated without orientation because the ordinary change-of-variables factor has the same law. A top form uses a signed determinant, so its signed integral requires a chosen orientation. The two transformation laws must be distinguished on nonorientable manifolds.

12.4 Manifolds with boundary

The upper and lower closed half-spaces are Hn={x∈Rn:xn≥0},H−n={x∈Rn:xn≤0}.\halfspace n=\{x\in\R^n:x^n\ge0\},\qquad \lowerhalfspace n=\{x\in\R^n:x^n\le0\}.Hn={x∈Rn:xn≥0},H−n​={x∈Rn:xn≤0}. A smooth manifold with boundary is Hausdorff and second countable, with charts onto relatively open subsets of either half-space. Allowing either side permits the coordinate reflection needed for positive charts also in dimension one. For example, at the right endpoint of the oriented interval [0,1][0,1][0,1], the positive chart u=x−1u=x-1u=x−1 maps into (−∞,0](-\infty,0](−∞,0]. Chart transitions and their inverses extend smoothly to neighbourhoods in Rn\R^nRn near each point. The boundary ∂M\boundary M∂M consists of chart points with xn=0x^n=0xn=0; the others form the interior. This geometric boundary differs from Lecture 6’s boundary operator on chains. The set of boundary points is independent of the chart. Indeed, the chain rule on interior points and continuity make transition derivatives invertible also at boundary points. The inverse function theorem then makes their extensions local diffeomorphisms. Such a map cannot send a half-neighbourhood to a full interior neighbourhood: its inverse would have to take points on both sides of the source hyperplane into the half-space. Restricting charts to xn=0x^n=0xn=0 gives charts of dimension n−1n-1n−1 on ∂M\boundary M∂M. The restricted transitions make them smoothly compatible. A transition’s last component vanishes on the boundary, so its tangential derivatives vanish there. Its normal derivative is nonzero by invertibility. It sends inward vectors to inward vectors because it sends interior points to interior points. Thus the outward transverse direction is chart-independent, even when the two charts use opposite half-spaces.

At a boundary point, TpMT_pMTp​M is the ambient nnn-dimensional space modeled by a half-space chart, with changes given by the transition Jacobians. Its vectors need not be velocities of two-sided curves remaining in MMM. In particular the outward vector −∂n-\partial_n−∂n​ is allowed in the upper model. Functions and forms are smooth when their chart coefficients extend smoothly to open sets of Rn\R^nRn. Their derivatives at the boundary are independent of extension, since extensions agree on the interior and all their derivatives agree there and extend continuously to the boundary. The same half-chart construction gives tangent changes and exterior differentiation. Partitions of unity and integration extend using restrictions of bumps to half-balls. The preceding normalization and independence proofs then apply.

On an oriented manifold the induced boundary orientation is outward first: a boundary basis (v1,…,vn−1)(v_1,\ldots,v_{n-1})(v1​,…,vn−1​) is positive if (ν,v1,…,vn−1)(\nu,v_1,\ldots,v_{n-1})(ν,v1​,…,vn−1​) is positive in TpMT_pMTp​M, where ν\nuν is outward transverse. Changing ν\nuν by a tangent vector or multiplying it by a positive scalar does not change this sign. This proves independence of that choice; no metric is required. For the standard orientation on Hn\halfspace nHn, take ν=−∂n\nu=-\partial_nν=−∂n​. The boundary orientation is represented by ι−∂n(dx1∧⋯∧dxn)=(−1)ndx1∧⋯∧dxn−1on xn=0.\contraction{-\partial_n}(\dd x^1\wedge\cdots\wedge\dd x^n) =(-1)^n\dd x^1\wedge\cdots\wedge\dd x^{n-1} \quad\text{on }x^n=0.ι−∂n​​(dx1∧⋯∧dxn)=(−1)ndx1∧⋯∧dxn−1on xn=0. On H−n\lowerhalfspace nH−n​ the outward vector is +∂n+\partial_n+∂n​, so the boundary orientation is (−1)n−1dx1∧⋯∧dxn−1(-1)^{n-1}\dd x^1\wedge\cdots\wedge\dd x^{n-1}(−1)n−1dx1∧⋯∧dxn−1. For n=1n=1n=1 the upper model gives the negative initial endpoint, and the lower model gives the positive final endpoint. The figure compares the upper half-plane with an interval.

The sphere orientation above is exactly the boundary orientation of the unit ball. The usual Möbius band with its boundary is also nonorientable, since an orientation would restrict to its nonorientable interior. A closed manifold means a compact manifold without boundary.

12.5 Stokes’ theorem in every dimension

Summary

Orientations fix signs of top forms. Partitions of unity define a chart-independent integral. Stokes applies to compactly supported forms on oriented manifolds with boundary, using outward-first boundary orientation. Its local proof is the fundamental theorem of calculus in the normal coordinate. Closed forms have equal fluxes across two oriented surfaces that together, with the second orientation reversed, bound a compact region. Removing a ball around the Coulomb singularity gives flux 4π4\pi4π. Lecture 13 studies closed forms modulo exact forms.
