Lecture 12

Orientation, integration and Stokes’ theorem

What this lecture does

An orientation specifies the sign of a top-degree form. It lets us integrate forms on a manifold. Stokes’ theorem identifies the integral of an exterior derivative with the integral on the boundary, using outward-first orientation. We prove it in every dimension and apply it to the flux of the Coulomb form.

Unit 12A Orientation and integration

12.1 Orientations and oriented charts

Definition 12.1 (Orientation of a vector space). For an nnn-dimensional real vector space with n≥1n\ge1n≥1, two ordered bases have the same orientation if their change-of-basis determinant is positive.

There are two classes.

Definition 12.2 (Smooth frame). A frame on an open set is an ordered list of smooth tangent fields that is a basis at every point.

Definition 12.3 (Orientation of a manifold). An orientation of a manifold chooses a class in each tangent space, locally represented by a smooth frame. A manifold is orientable if it admits such a choice, and oriented when a choice has been made.

Definition 12.4 (Oriented atlas). An oriented atlas has positive Jacobian determinants on all chart transitions.

Its coordinate frames give compatible orientation classes: the change of frame uses the inverse Jacobian, whose determinant is also positive. Conversely, near any point an orientation is represented by a frame. Its determinant relative to a coordinate frame is continuous and nonzero. Shrink to a connected chart so that the sign is constant. If necessary replace x1x^1x1 by −x1-x^1−x1. The coordinate frame then represents the chosen orientation. Charts obtained this way have positive transitions. This proves the equivalence of orientations and oriented atlases.

A nowhere-zero top form μ\volumeformμ selects the bases on which its value is positive. These choices are locally represented by coordinate frames, because its coefficient in a connected chart has constant sign.

Definition 12.5 (Orientation-preserving diffeomorphism). For oriented manifolds, a diffeomorphism is orientation-preserving when its differential sends positive bases to positive bases.

In positive charts this means its Jacobian determinant is positive.

For example, on Sn⊂Rn+1\sphere n\subset\R^{n+1}Sn⊂Rn+1 the form μp(v1,…,vn)=det⁡[p v1 ⋯ vn](vj∈TpSn)\volumeform_p(v_1,\ldots,v_n)=\det[p\ v_1\ \cdots\ v_n] \qquad(v_j\in T_p\sphere n)μp​(v1​,…,vn​)=det[p v1​ ⋯ vn​](vj​∈Tp​Sn) is smooth and nowhere zero: the radial vector ppp is independent of the nnn tangent basis vectors. It defines the outward orientation. For n=2n=2n=2 it is the sphere area form of Lecture 10. Lecture 10 computed the north-pole stereographic inverse pullback as −4du∧dv/(1+u2+v2)2-4\dd u\wedge\dd v/(1+u^2+v^2)^2−4du∧dv/(1+u2+v2)2. This chart reverses the outward orientation.

In dimension zero an orientation is a choice of sign +1+1+1 or −1-1−1 at each point. Integration of a function then means its signed sum. This convention includes the endpoints of an oriented interval.

12.2 Partitions of unity and orientability

A form may have support in several overlapping charts. We multiply it by nonnegative smooth functions whose sum is one. Each weighted form then has support inside one chart. A locally finite sum permits this construction on a noncompact manifold. Only finitely many pieces meet a compact support.

Definition 12.6 (Locally finite collection). A collection of sets is locally finite if every point has a neighbourhood meeting only finitely many of them.

Definition 12.7 (Partition of unity). A smooth partition of unity subordinate to an open cover {Ui}\{U_i\}{Ui​} consists of nonnegative smooth functions ρi\rho_iρi​ such that ∑iρi=1,supp⁡ρi⊂Ui,{supp⁡ρi} is locally finite.\sum_i\rho_i=1,\qquad \supp\rho_i\subset U_i, \qquad\{\supp\rho_i\}\text{ is locally finite}.i∑​ρi​=1,suppρi​⊂Ui​,{suppρi​} is locally finite. Several functions may be assigned to the same cover member.

Local finiteness makes each sum finite near any point. It also implies that a compact set meets only finitely many supports: choose finitely many such neighbourhoods covering the compact set.

Theorem 12.8 (Partitions of unitysketched; normalization and use proved). Every open cover of a smooth manifold admits a subordinate smooth partition of unity.

Sketch of existence. The second-countability assumption in Lecture 7 is used in this construction. Together with local compactness, it gives a countable exhaustion by compact sets, each lying in the interior of the next. Refining the cover on the successive compact layers gives a locally finite cover by coordinate balls with compact closure, with smaller balls still covering the manifold. Choose a smooth bump on each ball that is positive on its smaller ball and supported in the larger one. The coordinate construction uses the smooth function e−1/te^{-1/t}e−1/t for t>0t>0t>0, extended by zero for t≤0t\le0t≤0. 12.4 Manifolds with boundary

Three facts are used below. The boundary is a smooth manifold of dimension n−1n-1n−1. The outward transverse direction does not depend on the boundary chart. Tangent spaces at boundary points have dimension nnn, and forms can be differentiated there.

The closed disc, the closed ball and the annulus have boundary points with half-space neighbourhoods. Their interior points have ordinary open coordinate neighbourhoods. Allowing either half-space permits the coordinate reflection needed for positive charts also in dimension one. At the right endpoint of the oriented interval [0,1][0,1][0,1], the positive chart u=x−1u=x-1u=x−1 maps into (−∞,0](-\infty,0](−∞,0]. A positive chart increases with xxx, so it cannot send this right endpoint to zero with its interior on the positive side. Verification of the boundary facts

The set of boundary points is independent of the chart. Indeed, the chain rule on interior points and continuity make transition derivatives invertible also at boundary points. The inverse function theorem then makes their extensions local diffeomorphisms. Such a map cannot send a half-neighbourhood to a full interior neighbourhood: its inverse would have to take points on both sides of the source hyperplane into the half-space. Restricting charts to xn=0x^n=0xn=0 gives charts of dimension n−1n-1n−1 on ∂M\boundary M∂M. The restricted transitions make them smoothly compatible.

A transition’s last component vanishes on the boundary, so its tangential derivatives vanish there. Its normal derivative is nonzero by invertibility. It sends inward vectors to inward vectors because it sends interior points to interior points. Thus the outward transverse direction is chart-independent, even when the two charts use opposite half-spaces.

At a boundary point, TpMT_pMTp​M is the ambient nnn-dimensional space modeled by a half-space chart, with changes given by the transition Jacobians. Its vectors need not be velocities of two-sided curves remaining in MMM. In particular the outward vector −∂n-\partial_n−∂n​ is allowed in the upper model. Functions and forms are smooth when their chart coefficients extend smoothly to open sets of Rn\R^nRn. Their derivatives at the boundary are independent of extension, since extensions agree on the interior and all their derivatives agree there and extend continuously to the boundary. The same half-chart construction gives tangent changes and exterior differentiation. Partitions of unity and integration extend using restrictions of bumps to half-balls. The preceding normalization and independence proofs then apply.

On a coordinate cell the fundamental theorem of calculus gives the integral over its faces. Adding these formulas cancels internal faces. For a general manifold we prove the formula using partitions of unity. The cancellation is expressed by ∑idρi=0\sum_i\dd\rho_i=0∑i​dρi​=0.

Theorem 12.20 (Stokesproved given partitions of unity). Let MMM be an oriented smooth nnn-manifold with boundary, where n≥1n\ge1n≥1. For a smooth compactly supported (n−1)(n-1)(n−1)-form ω\formω, ∫Mdω=∫∂Mω.\int_M\dd\form=\int_{\boundary M}\form.∫M​dω=∫∂M​ω. The right side means restriction by the boundary inclusion, with the outward-first orientation. In particular the statement applies to every smooth (n−1)(n-1)(n−1)-form when MMM is compact.

Proof. First work on Hn\halfspace nHn with compact support. Write the form as ω=∑i=1n(−1)i−1fidx1∧⋯∧dxi^∧⋯∧dxn,dω=(∑i∂ifi)dx1∧⋯∧dxn.\form=\sum_{i=1}^n(-1)^{i-1}f_i\dd x^1\wedge\cdots \wedge\widehat{\dd x^i}\wedge\cdots\wedge\dd x^n, \qquad \dd\form=\Bigl(\sum_i\partial_i f_i\Bigr)\dd x^1\wedge\cdots\wedge\dd x^n.ω=i=1∑n​(−1)i−1fi​dx1∧⋯∧dxi∧⋯∧dxn,dω=(i∑​∂i​fi​)dx1∧⋯∧dxn. Fubini’s theorem and the fundamental theorem of calculus integrate ∂ifi\partial_i f_i∂i​fi​ first in xix^ixi. For i<ni<ni<n the endpoints are outside compact support, so each contribution is zero. The normal contribution is ∫Hndω=∫Rn−1∫0∞∂nfn(x′,xn) dxn dx′=−∫Rn−1fn(x′,0) dx′.\int_{\halfspace n}\dd\form =\int_{\R^{n-1}}\int_0^\infty\partial_n f_n(x',x^n)\,\dd x^n\,\dd x' =-\int_{\R^{n-1}}f_n(x',0)\,\dd x'.∫Hn​dω=∫Rn−1​∫0∞​∂n​fn​(x′,xn)dxndx′=−∫Rn−1​fn​(x′,0)dx′. On Rn\R^nRn all coordinate integrations have two endpoints outside support, so the integral of dω\dd\formdω is zero, matching its empty boundary. On H−n\lowerhalfspace nH−n​, integrating the normal coordinate from −∞-\infty−∞ to 000 gives +∫Rn−1fn(x′,0)dx′+\int_{\R^{n-1}}f_n(x',0)\dd x'+∫Rn−1​fn​(x′,0)dx′. Now choose positively oriented interior and boundary charts on MMM and a subordinate partition ρi\rho_iρi​. Each ρiω\rho_i\formρi​ω has compact support inside its chart. In coordinates it extends by zero across the artificial chart edges to a smooth compactly supported form on Rn\R^nRn, Hn\halfspace nHn or H−n\lowerhalfspace nH−n​. Here ∑iρi=1\sum_i\rho_i=1∑i​ρi​=1 and its differential is zero. The restricted partition also sums to one on the boundary. Thus the summed local equalities give the claimed global equality. 

The support hypothesis matters on a noncompact manifold. On R\RR, the improper integral of d(tanh⁡x)\dd(\tanh x)d(tanhx) is 1−(−1)=21-(-1)=21−(−1)=2, while the boundary is empty. The function has no compact support. The nonzero limiting values at infinity remain in the endpoint formula.

12.6 Classical integral theorems and equal fluxes

Definition 12.21 (Compact smooth region). A compact smooth region in Rn\R^nRn means a compact nnn-manifold with boundary embedded in Rn\R^nRn.

The outer boundary is counter-clockwise. Boundaries of holes are clockwise.

Summary

Orientations fix signs of top forms. Partitions of unity define a chart-independent integral. Stokes applies to compactly supported forms on oriented manifolds with boundary, using outward-first boundary orientation. Its local proof is the fundamental theorem of calculus in the normal coordinate. Closed forms have equal fluxes across two oriented surfaces that together, with the second orientation reversed, bound a compact region. Removing a ball around the Coulomb singularity gives flux 4π4\pi4π. Lecture 13 studies closed forms modulo exact forms.
