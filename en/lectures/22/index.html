Lecture 22

Chern classes and the Dirac monopole

What this lecture does

A connection can change while its bundle stays fixed. We extract a cohomology class from its curvature that survives this change. On a sphere its integral equals the transition winding and is therefore an integer. For a charged particle in a monopole field, this integer gives Dirac’s quantization condition. The tangent sphere supplies a second example through Gauss–Bonnet.

On the outward sphere its integral is the winding of the frame transition eS=eNgNSe_S=e_N\transition_{NS}eS​=eN​gNS​ along the northern boundary. The monopole condition is qΦm=2πℏn\charge\monopoleflux=2\pi\hbar nqΦm​=2πℏn with n∈Zn\in\Zn∈Z. The tangent sphere has Chern number 222, while O(n)\degreeline nO(n) has number nnn. A given complex bundle’s class needs no base metric or orientation. The tangent example uses an oriented metric to define its complex structure. Signed surface integrals need the orientation.
