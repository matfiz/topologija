Lecture 3

Homotopy and the fundamental group

What this lecture does

We compare paths by continuous deformations that keep their endpoints fixed. A path returning to its starting point is a loop. The classes of loops at one fixed point form the fundamental group. For the circle this group is the integers: a continuously chosen angle records how many turns a loop makes. We prove this by lifting paths and homotopies, then use the integer to prove the fundamental theorem of algebra.

Self-check.

Compute π1(S1×S1,(1,1))\fundamentalgroup(\sphere1\times\sphere1,(1,1))π1​(S1×S1,(1,1)) using the coordinate projections. Prove the group identification, not just its surjectivity.

Answer.

Send [(γ,δ)][(\gamma,\delta)][(γ,δ)] to (wind⁡γ,wind⁡δ)∈Z2(\wind\gamma,\wind\delta)\in\Z^2(windγ,windδ)∈Z2. Projecting a product homotopy gives endpoint-fixed homotopies of the coordinates. Winding is invariant under these, so the map is well-defined. Projection respects concatenation, so the map preserves multiplication coordinatewise. Conversely, the component test of Lecture 1 makes a pair of loops a loop in the product, and a pair of endpoint-fixed homotopies a homotopy in the product. Every (m,n)∈Z2(m,n)\in\Z^2(m,n)∈Z2 is represented by t↦(p(mt),p(nt))t\mapsto(p(mt),p(nt))t↦(p(mt),p(nt)). If both windings vanish, each coordinate loop has an endpoint-fixed contraction; their pair contracts the product loop. If two classes have the same image, their product with the inverse of the second has both windings zero, so is trivial. The two classes therefore agree. The group is Z2\Z^2Z2.

Summary

Classes of loops at one fixed base point form the fundamental group. Changing the base point along a path gives an isomorphism that can depend on that path. In a path-connected space, free homotopy keeps only conjugacy classes. Lifting computes π1(S1,1)≅Z\fundamentalgroup(\sphere1,1)\cong\Zπ1​(S1,1)≅Z; deformation retraction gives the same group for the punctured plane. This integer rules out a disc-to-circle retraction and proves the fundamental theorem of algebra. Lecture 4 extends the lifting construction to other maps.
