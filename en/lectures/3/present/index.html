Lecture 3

Homotopy and the fundamental group

We study which loops can be continuously deformed to a constant loop. A loop around a missing point in the plane cannot be contracted without meeting that point. We prove this obstruction by following a continuous phase around the circle. We compare paths by deformations that keep their endpoints fixed. A path returning to its starting point is a loop. The classes of loops at one fixed point form the fundamental group. For the circle this group is the integers: a continuously chosen angle records how many turns a loop makes. We prove this by lifting paths and homotopies, then use the integer to prove the fundamental theorem of algebra.

3.6 No retraction of the disc onto its circle

A retraction would assign a boundary point continuously to every point of the disc while fixing each boundary point. The obstruction is the loop that contracts through the disc but cannot contract along its boundary. Let D2={z∈C:∣z∣≤1}D^2=\{z\in\C:|z|\le1\}D2={z∈C:∣z∣≤1} be the closed disc.

Self-check.

State in one sentence what the fundamental group of (X,x0)(X,x_0)(X,x0​) is and why we introduced it. Explain why paths themselves are replaced by classes.

Answer.

The fundamental group is the group of endpoint-fixed homotopy classes of loops at x0x_0x0​, introduced to distinguish loops that cannot deform into one another while retaining concatenation. The two bracketings of three paths have different timings, but a change of timing is an endpoint-fixed homotopy. Thus associativity holds for classes.

Send [(γ,δ)][(\gamma,\delta)][(γ,δ)] to (wind⁡γ,wind⁡δ)∈Z2(\wind\gamma,\wind\delta)\in\Z^2(windγ,windδ)∈Z2. Projecting a product homotopy gives endpoint-fixed homotopies of the coordinates. Summary

Classes of loops at one fixed base point form the fundamental group. Changing the base point along a path gives an isomorphism that can depend on that path. In a path-connected space, free homotopy keeps only conjugacy classes. Lifting computes π1(S1,1)≅Z\fundamentalgroup(\sphere1,1)\cong\Zπ1​(S1,1)≅Z. Deformation retraction gives the same group for the punctured plane. This integer rules out a disc-to-circle retraction and proves the fundamental theorem of algebra. Lecture 4 extends the lifting construction to other maps.
