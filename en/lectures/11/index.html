Lecture 11

Cartan’s calculus

What this lecture does

Hamiltonian motion moves sets of initial positions and momenta through phase space. We prove that this motion preserves phase-space volume. Pullback compares a form at a moving point with a form at the starting point. Differentiating this comparison gives its Lie derivative. Cartan’s formula expresses that derivative using exterior differentiation and insertion of a vector field. We prove the formula, including its time-dependent version, and apply it to volume preservation and Hamiltonian motion.

11.6 Symplectic forms and Liouville’s theorem

The canonical 2-form in §11.1 is closed and nondegenerate. Closedness will make its Lie derivative vanish. Nondegeneracy makes the Hamiltonian equation determine a unique field.

Definition 11.17. On a 2n2n2n-manifold with n≥1n\ge1n≥1, a symplectic form ω\formω is a closed smooth 2-form that is nondegenerate: if ωp(v,w)=0\form_p(v,w)=0ωp​(v,w)=0 for every w∈TpMw\in T_pMw∈Tp​M, then v=0v=0v=0.

State in one sentence what the Lie derivative of a form is and why we introduced it. Explain how Cartan’s formula proves Hamiltonian volume preservation.

Answer.

The Lie derivative of a form is the infinitesimal change of its flow pullback, introduced to test how a flow changes or preserves that form. For a Hamiltonian field, Cartan gives LXHω=d2H+ιXHdω=0\Liederivative{\Hamiltonfield{\Hamiltonian}}\form =\dd^2\Hamiltonian+\contraction{\Hamiltonfield{\Hamiltonian}}\dd\form=0LXH​​ω=d2H+ιXH​​dω=0. Summary

Contraction inserts a vector in the first slot and is a graded antiderivation. The Lie derivative of forms differentiates flow pullback. Cartan expresses it as dιX+ιXd\dd\contraction X+\contraction X\dddιX​+ιX​d. A time-dependent form contributes its explicit time derivative as well. Zero divergence relative to a chosen volume form is equivalent to local flow preservation of that form. A Hamiltonian flow preserves its symplectic form and the associated top wedge volume.
