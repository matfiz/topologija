Lorentzian geometry and general relativity

What this lecture does

A Lorentzian metric specifies elapsed time and the paths of freely falling particles. Einstein’s equation relates its curvature to matter. We recover Newton’s acceleration in a weak field and verify the Schwarzschild vacuum metric. Its conserved quantities then reduce a massive particle’s motion to an orbit equation.

19.1 Lorentzian metrics and proper time

A Lorentzian spacetime is a smooth four-dimensional manifold with a metric of signature (+,−,−,−)(+,-,-,-)(+,−,−,−). At each point an orthonormal frame identifies the metric with η=diag⁡(1,−1,−1,−1)\Minkowski=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)η=diag(1,−1,−1,−1). This recalls the flat interval in Symmetries in Physics, Lecture 17, Minkowski space from physics. Here the metric may vary from point to point. Assume the spacetime is oriented, so its metric volume form is defined.

A nonzero tangent vector vvv is timelike if g(v,v)>0\metric(v,v)>0g(v,v)>0, null if g(v,v)=0\metric(v,v)=0g(v,v)=0, and spacelike if g(v,v)<0\metric(v,v)<0g(v,v)<0. The null vectors form the light cone. A time orientation is a continuous choice of one of the two timelike cones as future. We assume such a choice. In an orthonormal frame adapted to this choice, future vectors have positive time component. The figure shows one space direction and the time direction.

Use c=1c=1c=1 and coordinates x0=tx^0=tx0=t. The proper time along a future timelike curve x(σ)x(\sigma)x(σ) is defined by τ(σ1)−τ(σ0)=∫σ0σ1gμνdxμdσdxνdσ dσ.\tau(\sigma_1)-\tau(\sigma_0) =\int_{\sigma_0}^{\sigma_1} \sqrt{\metric_{\mu\nu}\frac{\dd x^\mu}{\dd\sigma} \frac{\dd x^\nu}{\dd\sigma}}\,\dd\sigma.τ(σ1​)−τ(σ0​)=∫σ0​σ1​​gμν​dσdxμ​dσdxν​​dσ. Its four-velocity U=dx/dτU=\dd x/\dd\tauU=dx/dτ satisfies g(U,U)=1\metric(U,U)=1g(U,U)=1. The physical free-fall postulate says that an unforced test particle follows a timelike geodesic of the Levi-Civita connection. A light ray follows a null geodesic, using an affine parameter in place of proper time. These are physical inputs. The existence and equation of these geodesics are the mathematical results of Lecture 16.
