27.1 The scalar kink and the Bogomolny bound

Definition 27.1. A static soliton is a spatially localized, finite-energy stationary solution of the field equations.

Here static means time independent. Localization concerns the energy density: the field itself may approach different nonzero vacua at the two ends.

27.2 Vacuum boundary data and sectors

Recall that a classical field configuration assigns a value to each spatial point. For a scalar field ϕ ⁣:Rd→RN\phi\colon\R^d\to\R^Nϕ:Rd→RN, take a smooth potential V≥0V\ge0V≥0. Its vacuum set is again V=V−1(0)\mathcal V=V^{-1}(0)V=V−1(0). A constant field in V\mathcal VV has zero potential and gradient energy.

In one dimension, assume limits ϕ−\phi_-ϕ−​ and ϕ+\phi_+ϕ+​ in V\mathcal VV at −∞-\infty−∞ and +∞+\infty+∞. Call their path components V−\mathcal V_-V−​ and V+\mathcal V_+V+​. In dimensions two and three, assume a continuous angular limit ϕ∞ ⁣:Sd−1→V\phi_\infty\colon S^{d-1}\to\mathcal Vϕ∞​:Sd−1→V, with convergence uniform in direction.

For an evolving field, require that its limiting boundary map be jointly continuous in angle and time. In one dimension require each endpoint limit to be continuous in time. The potential assigns positive energy density to values outside V\mathcal VV. This motivates vacuum limits, which we impose as boundary assumptions. Finite energy alone does not imply them. The vacuum manifold V\mathcal VV is the order-parameter space M\mathcal MM of Lecture 26 in these models. The sphere at infinity surrounds the entire localized region. A linking sphere from Lecture 26 carries the same class if it can be deformed to this sphere through a region on which the normalized field stays defined. In one dimension the sphere S0S^0S0 consists of the two distinguished endpoints. A homotopy of its maps moves each endpoint within its path component. Proposition 27.8 (Boundary sectorsproved). Under these boundary conditions, the one-dimensional datum is the ordered pair of path components of V\mathcal VV containing ϕ−\phi_-ϕ−​ and ϕ+\phi_+ϕ+​. In dimensions two and three it is the free homotopy class of ϕ∞\phi_\inftyϕ∞​. An evolution with jointly continuous vacuum boundary data preserves these data.

Proof. At either end in one dimension, the time-dependent limiting value is a path in V\mathcal VV. It cannot leave its path component. The two ends remain distinguished, giving an ordered pair. For d=2d=2d=2 or d=3d=3d=3, the jointly continuous boundary map is exactly a homotopy of maps Sd−1→VS^{d-1}\to\mathcal VSd−1→V. Its free homotopy class is therefore fixed. Lecture 26 expresses these classes using conjugacy or the fundamental-group action when needed. 

An integer charge exists in the kink and vortex examples, but the general sector datum need not be an integer. It constrains allowed deformations. Energy and the field equations determine whether a soliton exists.

A texture has different data: its value at infinity is fixed, and the degree belongs to the map on the compactified whole plane, as in Lecture 26. Its constant boundary map alone does not determine that degree.

Self-check.

For the scalar kink model, list the possible values of Q=(ϕ+−ϕ−)/(2v)\topcharge=(\phi_+-\phi_-)/(2v)Q=(ϕ+​−ϕ−​)/(2v) for vacuum endpoints. Count the ordered endpoint sectors and decide whether the charge distinguishes all of them.

Answer.

The four ordered pairs are (−v,−v)(-v,-v)(−v,−v), (−v,+v)(-v,+v)(−v,+v), (+v,−v)(+v,-v)(+v,−v) and (+v,+v)(+v,+v)(+v,+v). Their charges are respectively zero, +1+1+1, −1-1−1 and zero. Self-check.

State in one sentence what a static soliton is and why we introduced its energy. Explain why a nonzero charge is insufficient to identify one.

Answer.

A static soliton is a localized finite-energy stationary field, and its energy distinguishes stationary profiles and sector minima among fields with the same boundary topology. A charge labels allowed deformations, whereas the field equation must still be satisfied.

Summary

Continuous vacuum boundary data define sectors that an evolution with jointly continuous vacuum boundary data cannot change. Finite energy alone does not supply those limits. The scalar kink saturates E≥∣ΔW∣E\ge|\Delta W|E≥∣ΔW∣ and has signed charge +1+1+1. Derrick’s theorem excludes nonconstant smooth finite-energy static solutions of the stated nonnegative-potential linear-target scalar model for d≥2d\ge2d≥2. The gauge vortex lies outside that model and has flux 2πℏn/q2\pi\hbar n/\charge2πℏn/q under the stated asymptotic conditions. Its critical-coupling bound follows by square completion. Existence uses the cited analytic input. Lecture 28 uses a quantized sphere flux to count chiral quantum zero modes.
