27.1 Vacuum boundary data and sectors

Recall that a classical field configuration assigns a value to each spatial point. For a scalar field ϕ ⁣:Rd→RN\phi\colon\R^d\to\R^Nϕ:Rd→RN, take a smooth potential V≥0V\ge0V≥0. Its zero set V=V−1(0)\mathcal V=V^{-1}(0)V=V−1(0) is the vacuum manifold when it is a manifold. A constant field in V\mathcal VV has zero potential and gradient energy. A static soliton is a spatially localized, finite-energy stationary solution of the field equations. The boundary conditions below specify the allowed behaviour far from its localized region. A configuration carrying a nonzero topological charge is not automatically such a solution.

In one dimension, assume limits ϕ−\phi_-ϕ−​ and ϕ+\phi_+ϕ+​ in V\mathcal VV at −∞-\infty−∞ and +∞+\infty+∞. Call their path components V−\mathcal V_-V−​ and V+\mathcal V_+V+​. In dimensions two and three, assume a continuous angular limit ϕ∞ ⁣:Sd−1→V\phi_\infty\colon S^{d-1}\to\mathcal Vϕ∞​:Sd−1→V, with convergence uniform in direction. A soliton sector fixes the corresponding boundary homotopy data. For an evolving field, require that its limiting boundary map be jointly continuous in angle and time. In one dimension require each endpoint limit to be continuous in time. These are boundary assumptions. Finite energy alone does not imply them. The figure preserves the order of the one-dimensional endpoints.

Proposition 27.1 (Boundary sectorsproved). Under these boundary conditions, the one-dimensional datum is the ordered pair of path components of V\mathcal VV containing ϕ−\phi_-ϕ−​ and ϕ+\phi_+ϕ+​. In dimensions two and three it is the free homotopy class of ϕ∞\phi_\inftyϕ∞​. An evolution with jointly continuous vacuum boundary data preserves these data.

Proof. At either end in one dimension, the time-dependent limiting value is a path in V\mathcal VV. It cannot leave its path component. The two ends remain distinguished, giving an ordered pair. For d=2d=2d=2 or d=3d=3d=3, the jointly continuous boundary map is exactly a homotopy of maps Sd−1→VS^{d-1}\to\mathcal VSd−1→V. Its free homotopy class is therefore fixed. Lecture 26 expresses these classes using conjugacy or the fundamental-group action when needed. 

A topological charge is an invariant used to label such a sector. An integer charge exists in the examples below, but the general sector datum need not be an integer. It constrains allowed deformations. Energy and the field equations determine whether a soliton exists.

27.5 The monopole charge

Summary

Continuous vacuum boundary data define sectors that an evolution with jointly continuous vacuum boundary data cannot change. Finite energy alone does not supply those limits. The scalar kink saturates E≥∣ΔW∣E\ge|\Delta W|E≥∣ΔW∣ and has signed charge +1+1+1. Derrick’s theorem excludes nonconstant smooth finite-energy static solutions of the stated nonnegative-potential linear-target scalar model for d≥2d\ge2d≥2. The gauge vortex lies outside that model and has flux 2πℏn/q2\pi\hbar n/\charge2πℏn/q under the stated asymptotic conditions. Its critical-coupling bound follows by square completion. Existence uses the cited analytic input. Lecture 28 uses a quantized sphere flux to count chiral quantum zero modes.
