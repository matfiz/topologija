Lecture 24

Berry’s phase

A slowly cycled Hamiltonian can return an eigenstate with a measurable relative phase. Part of this phase depends on the elapsed time. Another part depends on the path of the parameters. We identify the second part with line-bundle holonomy. For the aligned spin-1/21/21/2 ground state, it is minus half the signed solid angle enclosed by the field-direction loop. The eigenvalue gap states where this description applies.

Answer.

It changes by −[χ(C(1))−χ(C(0))]-[\chi(C(1))-\chi(C(0))]−[χ(C(1))−χ(C(0))]. Thus this integral alone is not an intrinsic open-path phase. Summary

A smooth simple eigenline has the unitary connection A=⟨n|dn⟩\Berryconn=\braket n{\dd n}A=⟨n∣dn⟩ and curvature ⟨dn∣∧∣dn⟩\bra{\dd n}\wedge\ket{\dd n}⟨dn∣∧∣dn⟩. The cited gapped adiabatic theorem identifies its holonomy with the geometric factor after removing the dynamical phase. The imaginary connection and real potential satisfy AB=iA\Berryreal=\ii\BerryconnAB​=iA. For H=−B⋅σH=-\mathbf B\cdot\boldsymbol{\Pauli}H=−B⋅σ with B≠0\mathbf B\ne0B=0, the ground line has Chern number −1-1−1 on an outward direction sphere and γ=−Ω/2\Berryphase=-\solidangle/2γ=−Ω/2 modulo 2π2\pi2π. The excluded degeneracy is the source of this parameter-space curvature. Lecture 25 integrates the same curvature over the momentum torus of a gapped band.
