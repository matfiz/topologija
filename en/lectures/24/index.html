Lecture 24

Berry’s phase

What this lecture does

A slowly cycled Hamiltonian can return an eigenstate with a measurable relative phase. Part of this phase depends on the elapsed time. Another part depends on the path of the parameters. We identify the second part with line-bundle holonomy. For the aligned spin-1/21/21/2 ground state, it is minus half the signed solid angle enclosed by the field-direction loop. The eigenvalue gap states where this description applies.

24.1 Slow evolution and the gap

Recall the finite-dimensional quantum notation from Quantum Mechanics I. A pure state is a complex line in CN\C^NCN. A unit representative is written as a ket ∣ψ⟩\ket\psi∣ψ⟩. Its conjugate transpose is the bra ⟨ψ∣\bra\psi⟨ψ∣, a linear functional. The inner product ⟨ϕ|ψ⟩=ϕ†ψ\braket\phi\psi=\phi^\dagger\psi⟨ϕ∣ψ⟩=ϕ†ψ is antilinear in its first argument. 24.2 Eigenlines and their connection

Let U\mathcal UU be an open region in a smooth parameter manifold, with a smooth family H ⁣:U→Herm⁡(N)H\colon\mathcal U\to\operatorname{Herm}(N)H:U→Herm(N) and a chosen smooth simple eigenvalue En(R)E_n(R)En​(R). Here Herm⁡(N)\operatorname{Herm}(N)Herm(N) is the real vector space of Hermitian matrices.

Physical input.

In an ideal coherent two-arm interferometer, the complex amplitudes of the arms add at recombination. The detection intensity is proportional to the squared modulus of their sum. We assume that both arms end in the same internal eigenline with the same normalized representative.

We cycle the parameters in one arm and hold them fixed in the reference arm. If their energy histories have equal integrals and their durations agree, their dynamical phases cancel in the relative phase. For arm amplitudes acyca_{\mathrm{cyc}}acyc​ and arefa_{\mathrm{ref}}aref​, the detection intensity is proportional to ∣acyceiγ(C)+aref∣2=∣acyc∣2+∣aref∣2+2Re⁡(aref‾acyceiγ(C)).|a_{\mathrm{cyc}} e^{\ii\Berryphase(C)}+a_{\mathrm{ref}}|^2 =|a_{\mathrm{cyc}}|^2+|a_{\mathrm{ref}}|^2 +2\operatorname{Re}\bigl(\overline{a_{\mathrm{ref}}}a_{\mathrm{cyc}}e^{\ii\Berryphase(C)}\bigr).∣acyc​eiγ(C)+aref​∣2=∣acyc​∣2+∣aref​∣2+2Re(aref​​acyc​eiγ(C)). 24.5 The degeneracy and general spin

Summary

A smooth simple eigenline has the unitary connection A=⟨n|dn⟩\Berryconn=\braket n{\dd n}A=⟨n∣dn⟩ and curvature ⟨dn∣∧∣dn⟩\bra{\dd n}\wedge\ket{\dd n}⟨dn∣∧∣dn⟩. The cited gapped adiabatic theorem identifies its holonomy with the geometric factor after removing the dynamical phase. The imaginary connection and real potential satisfy AB=iA\Berryreal=\ii\BerryconnAB​=iA. For H=−B⋅σH=-\mathbf B\cdot\boldsymbol{\Pauli}H=−B⋅σ with B≠0\mathbf B\ne0B=0, the ground line has Chern number −1-1−1 on an outward direction sphere and γ=−Ω/2\Berryphase=-\solidangle/2γ=−Ω/2 modulo 2π2\pi2π. The excluded degeneracy is the source of this parameter-space curvature. Lecture 25 integrates the same curvature over the momentum torus of a gapped band.
