Lecture 15

Metrics, the Hodge star and Maxwell’s equations

What this lecture does

A metric measures tangent vectors and selects a volume form once an orientation is chosen. The Hodge star then turns a form into a form of complementary degree. On spacetime this operation packages Maxwell’s four equations into two equations for forms. The monopole flux shows why closed field forms need not have global potentials.

15.1 Metrics and the identification of vectors with covectors

Let MMM be a smooth nnn-manifold. A Riemannian metric g\metricg is a smoothly varying positive-definite symmetric bilinear form gp\metric_pgp​ on each tangent space TpMT_pMTp​M. We use g\metricg for the metric throughout Part II.

By Sylvester’s law of inertia, a nondegenerate symmetric form admits a basis with diagonal entries +1+1+1 and −1-1−1. Such a basis is called pseudo-orthonormal. Here a Lorentzian metric in dimension n≥2n\ge2n≥2 has signature (1,n−1)(1,n-1)(1,n−1).

15.2 Volume forms and the Hodge star

On an oriented pseudo-Riemannian nnn-manifold, the metric volume form is, in positive coordinates, μ=∣det⁡(gij)∣ dx1∧⋯∧dxn.\volumeform=\sqrt{|\det(\metric_{ij})|}\,\dd x^1\wedge\cdots\wedge\dd x^n.μ=∣det(gij​)∣​dx1∧⋯∧dxn. They are smooth because the determinant never vanishes. This is the chosen volume form μ\volumeformμ of Lecture 11, now fixed by metric and orientation. Without orientation the same coefficient defines a density.

For 0≤k≤n0\le k\le n0≤k≤n, the inverse metric gives a bilinear pairing of covectors. Its induced pairing on decomposable kkk-forms is ⟨α1∧⋯∧αk,β1∧⋯∧βk⟩g=det⁡[g−1(αi,βj)]i,j=1k,\langle\alpha_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\alpha_k, \beta_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\beta_k\rangle_\metric =\det\bigl[\metric^{-1}(\alpha_i,\beta_j)\bigr]_{i,j=1}^k,⟨α1​∧⋯∧αk​,β1​∧⋯∧βk​⟩g​=det[g−1(αi​,βj​)]i,j=1k​, extended bilinearly. This uses Lecture 10’s determinant wedge, with no additional normalization from the fermionic wedge of Quantum Mechanics I. The pairing is nondegenerate: in a pseudo-orthonormal dual basis the basis wedges are orthogonal and each has norm squared +1+1+1 or −1-1−1.

Summary

A Riemannian metric exists by partitions of unity and identifies tangent vectors with covectors. A metric and orientation define volume and the Hodge star. Its square depends on degree, dimension and the number of negative metric directions. With signature +−−−+---+−−−, Maxwell’s equations are dFem=0\dd\Fem=0dFem​=0 and d⋆Fem=J\dd\HodgeStar\Fem=\currentformd⋆Fem​=J. They imply charge conservation and local potentials. Monopole flux prevents a global potential. On closed oriented Riemannian manifolds, the cited Hodge theorem selects one harmonic form in each cohomology class.
