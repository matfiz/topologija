Lecture 15

Metrics, the Hodge star and Maxwell’s equations

A metric measures tangent vectors and selects a volume form once an orientation is chosen. The Hodge star then turns a form into a form of complementary degree. On spacetime this operation packages Maxwell’s four equations into two equations for forms. The monopole flux shows why closed field forms need not have global potentials. On a closed oriented Riemannian manifold, the metric selects a harmonic representative of each de Rham class.

Summary

A Riemannian metric exists by partitions of unity and identifies tangent vectors with covectors. A metric and orientation define volume and the Hodge star. Its square depends on degree, dimension and the number of negative metric directions. With signature +−−−+---+−−−, Maxwell’s equations are dFem=0\dd\Fem=0dFem​=0 and d⋆Fem=J\dd\HodgeStar\Fem=\currentformd⋆Fem​=J. They imply charge conservation and local potentials. Monopole flux prevents a global potential. On closed oriented Riemannian manifolds, the cited Hodge theorem selects one harmonic form in each cohomology class.
