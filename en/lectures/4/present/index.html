Lecture 4

Covering spaces and rotations

The circle projection of Lecture 3 has local inverse branches. A covering map has the same property over every sufficiently small open set. We lift paths using these branches and recover loop classes from their endpoints above a fixed point. The antipodal covering then computes the fundamental group of the rotation space: a full turn represents the nontrivial class, and two full turns represent the identity.

4.1 Coverings and local inverse branches

The symbol ∐\coprod∐ denotes a disjoint union. The sets VαV_\alphaVα​ are open in X~\widetilde XX. We call UUU evenly covered and each VαV_\alphaVα​ a sheet over UUU.

The fibre p−1({x})p^{-1}(\{x\})p−1({x}) is the inverse image of the point, as in Lecture 2. It contains one point in each sheet over UUU. In particular, the fibre has the discrete subspace topology: every one of its points is open in the fibre. Its cardinality is constant as xxx varies in an evenly covered set. The figure shows the local structure.

For example, the projection u↦e2πiuu\mapsto e^{2\pi\ii u}u↦e2πiu from R\RR to S1\sphere1S1 is a covering. 4.2 Lifting paths and their homotopies

Recall that a lift of f ⁣:Y→Xf\colon Y\to Xf:Y→X is a continuous map f~ ⁣:Y→X~\tilde f\colon Y\to\widetilde Xf~​:Y→X with p∘f~=fp\circ\tilde f=fp∘f~​=f. Write I=[0,1]I=[0,1]I=[0,1].

Theorem 4.2 (Path and path-homotopy lifting). For a covering ppp, a path γ ⁣:I→X\gamma\colon I\to Xγ:I→X has a unique lift after specifying γ~(0)\tilde\gamma(0)γ~​(0) above γ(0)\gamma(0)γ(0). A homotopy h ⁣:I2→Xh\colon I^2\to Xh:I2→X has a unique lift after a lift of its bottom edge has been specified. If hhh fixes its path endpoints, so does its lift.

Proof. First, uniqueness of a path lift follows locally. On a subinterval mapping into an evenly covered UUU, any lift stays in one sheet: the interval is connected and the sheets are disjoint and open. The sheet is determined by one lifted value. Its inverse branch determines the whole subinterval.

For existence, pull back evenly covered sets to an open cover of III. The Lebesgue-number lemma of Lemma 3.5 gives a finite subdivision such that each closed subinterval maps into one such set. On the first subinterval use the sheet containing the prescribed initial point. On each successive subinterval use the sheet containing the preceding endpoint. The formulas agree at the joins and paste continuously. Local uniqueness on successive subintervals gives uniqueness on III.

For a homotopy, pull the same open cover back to the compact square. Choose a grid with each cell mapping into an evenly covered set. Extend the bottom lift row by row, from left to right. Choose on each cell the inverse branch whose sheet contains the already assigned lower-left value. On its bottom and left edges, path-lift uniqueness forces agreement with every previously assigned value. There are no other previously assigned points on the cell. Finite pasting gives a continuous lift on the square. The same cell order proves uniqueness. This is the grid construction of Lemma 3.6, with sheets in place of integer translates.

Finally, if an edge of hhh is constant, its lift is a path in a discrete fibre. A continuous image of the connected interval is connected, so this path is constant. Apply this to the two endpoint edges. 

In particular, endpoint-fixed homotopic paths have lifts with the same endpoint when their initial lifts agree. The induced homomorphism p∗p_*p∗​ is injective: lift a based contraction of a projected loop. The lift is a based contraction of the original loop. Also, [γ]∈p∗π1(X~,x~0)⟺the lift of γ from x~0 closes.[\gamma]\in p_*\fundamentalgroup(\widetilde X,\tilde x_0) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \text{the lift of $\gamma$ from $\tilde x_0$ closes}.[γ]∈p∗​π1​(X,x~0​)⟺the lift of γ from x~0​ closes. 4.3 Fibres, loop classes and deck transformations

Definition 4.3 (Deck transformation). A deck transformation of ppp is a homeomorphism D ⁣:X~→X~D\colon\widetilde X\to\widetilde XD:X→X with p∘D=pp\circ D=pp∘D=p. Their group under composition is written Deck⁡(p)\Deck(p)Deck(p).

On a path-connected covering space, a deck transformation is determined by its value at one point. Indeed, for a path aaa from that point to any other point, D∘aD\circ aD∘a is the unique lift of p∘ap\circ ap∘a from the specified image point. In particular, a deck transformation fixing one point is the identity. The deck group acts transitively on a fibre if for any two points of that fibre some deck transformation sends the first to the second.

Corollary 4.4 (Counting a simply connected covering). Let p ⁣:X~→Xp\colon\widetilde X\to Xp:X→X be a covering with X~\widetilde XX simply connected. Fix x~0\tilde x_0x~0​ above x0x_0x0​. Sending a loop class to its lifted endpoint gives a bijection π1(X,x0)⟶p−1({x0}).\fundamentalgroup(X,x_0)\longrightarrow p^{-1}(\{x_0\}).π1​(X,x0​)⟶p−1({x0​}). If the deck group acts transitively on this fibre, it is isomorphic to π1(X,x0)\fundamentalgroup(X,x_0)π1​(X,x0​).

Proof. The endpoint depends only on the based class by homotopy lifting. Every fibre point can be joined to x~0\tilde x_0x~0​, since simple connectedness includes path-connectedness. Projecting this path proves surjectivity. If two lifted paths have the same endpoint, join the first to the reverse of the second. This loop contracts in X~\widetilde XX. Its projection shows that the two original loop classes agree. This proves injectivity.

Under transitivity, let DγD_\gammaDγ​ be the unique deck transformation taking x~0\tilde x_0x~0​ to the endpoint of the lift γ~\tilde\gammaγ~​. Thus the bijection preserves multiplication with the course’s first-path-first convention. The isomorphism uses the chosen lifted base point.

4.4 The antipodal cover and rotations

Proposition 4.5 (Spheres of dimension at least twosketched). The sphere Sn\sphere nSn is simply connected for n≥2n\ge2n≥2.

Proof sketch. Short spherical arcs join nonantipodal points. A path via a third point joins antipodes, so the sphere is path-connected.

First, cover the loop by open hemispheres. Compactness and Lemma 3.5 give a finite subdivision whose pieces each lie in one hemisphere. Replace each piece by the shortest spherical arc joining its endpoints in that hemisphere. Normalizing their straight interpolation gives the homotopy: its scalar product with the hemisphere centre stays positive.

Next, the resulting polygon lies in finitely many two-dimensional linear subspaces. For n≥2n\ge2n≥2 these are proper subspaces of Rn+1\R^{n+1}Rn+1 and their union misses a point of the sphere. Finally, stereographic projection from that point identifies its complement with Rn\R^nRn, where the polygon contracts with its base point fixed. The formulas of Lecture 1 apply in nnn coordinates. This sketches the sphere result of Hatcher, Proposition 1.14, using polygonal approximation. The original continuous loop need not miss a point.

Recall from Lecture 2 that RPn\RP nRPn identifies antipodal points of Sn\sphere nSn. The quotient map is a covering. To see this, for x∈Snx\in\sphere nx∈Sn take the cap V={u∈Sn:u⋅x>1/2}V=\{u\in\sphere n:u\cdot x>1/2\}V={u∈Sn:u⋅x>1/2}. The inverse image of its quotient image is V∐(−V)V\coprod(-V)V∐(−V). The two caps are disjoint. The quotient map is open, because the saturation of any open set WWW is the open set W∪(−W)W\cup(-W)W∪(−W). Each cap therefore maps homeomorphically onto the same open quotient set. These sets cover RPn\RP nRPn.

Write Z2\Z_2Z2​ for the integers modulo two.

Theorem 4.6 (Projective space and rotationsproved given Sn\sphere nSn simply connected). For n≥2n\ge2n≥2, the antipodal cover gives π1(RPn)≅Z2\fundamentalgroup(\RP n)\cong\Z_2π1​(RPn)≅Z2​. Consequently π1(SO(3))≅Z2\fundamentalgroup(\SO(3))\cong\Z_2π1​(SO(3))≅Z2​.

Proof. The sphere is simply connected by Proposition 4.5, at the sketched level just given. The identity and antipodal map act transitively on the two-point fibre. Deck uniqueness excludes any other transformation. The antipodal map has square the identity, so Corollary 4.4 gives Z2\Z_2Z2​. The homeomorphism RP3≅SO(3)\RP3\cong\SO(3)RP3≅SO(3) was proved in Proposition 2.9 by the axis–angle construction. 

The circle case is different: SO(2)≅S1\SO(2)\cong\sphere1SO(2)≅S1 has fundamental group Z\ZZ. The general result π1(SO(m))≅Z2\fundamentalgroup(\SO(m))\cong\Z_2π1​(SO(m))≅Z2​ for m≥3m\ge3m≥3 is cited from Nakahara, Table 4.1; only m=3m=3m=3 is proved in this lecture.

4.5 Universal covers and their classification

A simply connected covering space is called a universal cover. A space is semilocally simply connected if each x∈Xx\in Xx∈X has an open neighbourhood UUU such that every loop in UUU based at xxx contracts in XXX. The neighbourhood itself need not be simply connected. A covering isomorphism is a homeomorphism between the total spaces commuting with their projections to XXX. In the based version it also preserves the chosen lifted point. Two subgroups are conjugate if they have the form HHH and k−1Hkk^{-1}Hkk−1Hk for some group element kkk.

Theorem 4.7 (Existence and classificationcited). Let XXX be nonempty, connected, locally path-connected and semilocally simply connected. It has a universal cover. Isomorphism classes of based connected coverings correspond to subgroups of π1(X,x0)\fundamentalgroup(X,x_0)π1​(X,x0​) by p∗π1(X~,x~0)p_*\fundamentalgroup(\widetilde X,\tilde x_0)p∗​π1​(X,x~0​). Without chosen base points, they correspond to conjugacy classes of subgroups.

This is Hatcher, Theorem 1.38. Local path-connectedness makes path components open, so connectedness implies path-connectedness here, both downstairs and in a connected cover.

Self-check.

Classify the connected coverings of the circle up to covering isomorphism.

Answer.

The circle satisfies the classification theorem’s hypotheses: small open arcs are intervals. Every subgroup of Z\ZZ is mZm\ZmZ for exactly one integer m≥0m\ge0m≥0. For a nonzero subgroup, its least positive element mmm and integer division prove this; the zero subgroup is 0Z0\Z0Z. For m≥1m\ge1m≥1, the map z↦zmz\mapsto z^mz↦zm on S1\sphere1S1 induces multiplication by mmm on winding, so its image subgroup is mZm\ZmZ. The cover R→S1\R\to\sphere1R→S1 has subgroup zero because R\RR is contractible. The cited classification proves these exhaust the connected coverings. All subgroups are their own conjugacy classes, since Z\ZZ is abelian.

Summary

Local inverse sheets lift paths and their homotopies uniquely. For a simply connected covering space, based loop classes correspond to fibre points. A transitive deck group gives the same group structure after choosing a lifted base point. The antipodal cover computes π1(RPn)≅Z2\fundamentalgroup(\RP n) \cong\Z_2π1​(RPn)≅Z2​ for n≥2n\ge2n≥2, using the sketched sphere result. In SO(3)\SO(3)SO(3), a full turn is nontrivial and a double turn is trivial. Lecture 5 computes fundamental groups by joining open pieces.
