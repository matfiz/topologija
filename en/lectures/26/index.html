Lecture 26

Higher homotopy groups and topological defects

What this lecture does

A loop detects whether a line defect can be removed while the surrounding medium stays ordered. A sphere provides the corresponding test for a point defect. We extend the fundamental group to maps from higher-dimensional spheres. We then distinguish based charges from the free homotopy classes that describe local defects. This distinction identifies opposite point charges for unoriented axes, but preserves their sign for oriented magnetic directions.

26.1 Ordered values and two defects

Definition 26.1 (Order parameter). An order parameter records the local ordered state of a medium.

Definition 26.2 (Order-parameter space). The allowed ordered values form the order-parameter space M\mathcal MM.

When a symmetry group GGG acts transitively on the uniform ordered states, Lecture 8 identifies this space with G/HG/HG/H, where HHH fixes a reference state.

Definition 26.3 (Director). A director is an unoriented unit axis: n\mathbf nn and −n-\mathbf n−n describe the same state.

Definition 26.4 (Texture). The word texture means a continuous spatial configuration of the order parameter.

Physical input.

We model a superfluid by a complex field whose ordered value is its phase in S1S^1S1. We model a Heisenberg ferromagnet by a unit direction in S2S^2S2. We model a uniaxial nematic by a director in RP2\RP2RP2. These descriptions restrict the field to ordered values away from defect cores.

Definition 26.5 (Defect core). A defect core is a region where the chosen ordered description fails.

Outside the cores the order parameter is a continuous map into M\mathcal MM.

Definition 26.6 (Linking circle and sphere). A small circle surrounding a line in three-dimensional space is a linking circle. A small sphere surrounding an isolated point is its linking sphere.

Definition 26.7 (Codimension and transverse disk). The codimension of a smooth core is the spatial dimension minus the core dimension. A transverse disk meets the core at one point in directions complementary to its tangent directions.

The figure separates these two domains of the order-parameter map.

For a straight superfluid core, a counter-clockwise linking circle can carry ϕ↦eiϕ\phi\mapsto e^{\ii\phi}ϕ↦eiϕ. Its winding is +1+1+1 by Lecture 3. It cannot extend to a continuous phase on the transverse disk because such an extension would contract that loop. Its degree is +1+1+1 by Lecture 14. We need sphere maps to describe this point obstruction.

A model energy explains the restriction to ordered values.

26.3 Sphere groups and the Hopf map

The degree from Lecture 14 is invariant under homotopy. The converse for sphere maps is an additional theorem: equal degrees imply based homotopy. We cite it here rather than infer it from the integral formula alone.

Theorem 26.11 (Homotopy groups of spherescited). For every n≥1n\ge1n≥1, the degree map gives πn(Sn)≅Z\pi_n(S^n)\cong\Zπn​(Sn)≅Z. For 1≤k<n1\le k<n1≤k<n, the group πk(Sn)\pi_k(S^n)πk​(Sn) vanishes. The group π3(S2)\pi_3(S^2)π3​(S2) is infinite cyclic and is generated by the Hopf map S3→S2S^3\to S^2S3→S2 of Lecture 20.

The precise sources are Hatcher, Corollary 4.9, p. 349, Corollary 4.25, p. 361, and Example 4.51, p. 380. The degree is Lecture 14’s integral degree.1 Thus the identity sphere map has charge +1+1+1 and generates πn(Sn)\pi_n(S^n)πn​(Sn). For smooth representatives constant near the cube boundary, choose a common regular value different from x0x_0x0​. Its preimages in f+gf+gf+g lie in the two half-cubes. Both first-coordinate rescalings preserve orientation. The signed preimage counts therefore give deg⁡(f+g)=deg⁡f+deg⁡g.\mapdegree(f+g)=\mapdegree f+\mapdegree g.deg(f+g)=degf+degg. We first compress the cube map into its interior to make it constant near the boundary. We then use smooth approximation relative to that neighbourhood (Lee, Theorem 6.26, recalled in Lecture 13).

A smooth map Sk→SnS^k\to S^nSk→Sn with k<nk<nk<n misses a point: its entire image consists of critical values and has measure zero by Sard’s theorem recalled in Lecture 14. The complement of a point in SnS^nSn is contractible by stereographic projection. The contraction can fix the image basepoint, so the map is based null-homotopic. Smoothing supplies the same conclusion for continuous maps. There is no missing-point argument for the Hopf map, which is onto S2S^2S2. Its nontrivial class in π3(S2)\pi_3(S^2)π3​(S2) is the cited higher-dimensional example.

Lecture 3’s winding belongs to π1(S1)\pi_1(S^1)π1​(S1). Lecture 14’s index of a zero in dimension n≥2n\ge2n≥2 is the degree of a map in πn−1(Sn−1)\pi_{n-1}(S^{n-1})πn−1​(Sn−1). Lecture 22’s transition winding belongs to π1(U(1))\pi_1(\U(1))π1​(U(1)). Lecture 23’s integer kkk is the degree of the asymptotic map S3→SU(2)S^3\to\SU(2)S3→SU(2), a class in π3(SU(2))≅Z\pi_3(\SU(2))\cong\Zπ3​(SU(2))≅Z under that lecture’s orientation convention.

We will also use the covering-space result in Hatcher, Proposition 4.1, p. 342: a covering induces an isomorphism on πn\pi_nπn​ for n≥2n\ge2n≥2. Sphere maps and their homotopies lift, which explains this result. The contractible cover R→S1\R\to S^1R→S1 gives πn(S1)=0\pi_n(S^1)=0πn​(S1)=0 for n≥2n\ge2n≥2. 26.4 The fibration sequence and two consequences

Definition 26.12 (Fibration). A fibration is a continuous map p ⁣:E→Bp\colon E\to Bp:E→B with the homotopy lifting property: for every space YYY, a homotopy Y×[0,1]→BY\times[0,1]\to BY×[0,1]→B with a chosen lift at its initial time has a continuous lift throughout that restricts to the chosen lift at time zero.

For the theorem below it suffices to require this property for disks of every dimension. Fibre bundles have this property for disks by Hatcher’s Proposition 4.48, p. 379. Choose a basepoint b0∈Bb_0\in Bb0​∈B and a point e0e_0e0​ in its fibre F=p−1(b0)F=p^{-1}(b_0)F=p−1(b0​). All homotopy groups below use these compatible basepoints.

26.5 Nematic lifts and moving basepoints

A director map from a linking S2S^2S2 lifts to the internal unit-vector sphere because its domain is simply connected, by Lecture 4’s lifting theorem. There are exactly two lifts, f~\widetilde ff​ and −f~-\widetilde f−f​. They are maps into the internal sphere, not tangent vector fields on the physical sphere. The antipodal map on the target has degree −1-1−1, so these lifts have degrees ddd and −d-d−d. The physical linking sphere keeps its outward orientation. The radial director field therefore has both an outward and an inward vector lift. The spatial comparison loop in the figure carries a half-turn of the director. The figure separates the source orientation from the target sign.

For an ordinary based charge, a chosen point of the domain sphere must have a prescribed image x0x_0x0​. A deformation of the physical texture can move that image. We therefore need the effect of moving the basepoint. Let γ ⁣:[0,1]→M\gamma\colon[0,1]\to\mathcal Mγ:[0,1]→M run from x0x_0x0​ to x1x_1x1​. Rescale the domain of a cube map based at x1x_1x1​ onto a smaller concentric cube. On each radial segment of the surrounding shell, insert γ\gammaγ, running from x0x_0x0​ at the outer boundary to x1x_1x1​ at the inner boundary. The figure depicts this construction.

Proposition 26.14 (The basepoint actionsketched). The shell construction gives an isomorphism βγ ⁣:πn(M,x1)→πn(M,x0)\beta_\gamma\colon\pi_n(\mathcal M,x_1)\to\pi_n(\mathcal M,x_0)βγ​:πn​(M,x1​)→πn​(M,x0​). Theorem 26.16 (Local defect classessketched). Let M\mathcal MM be a path-connected order-parameter manifold. Classify restrictions to a linking domain by continuous homotopy through maps into M\mathcal MM. Without a fixed image basepoint, line defects in three dimensions have the conjugacy classes of π1(M)\pi_1(\mathcal M)π1​(M), and point defects have the orbits of π2(M)\pi_2(\mathcal M)π2​(M) under the π1(M)\pi_1(\mathcal M)π1​(M) action. A two-dimensional texture with a fixed uniform value at spatial infinity has a based class in π2(M)\pi_2(\mathcal M)π2​(M).

Sketch. The first assertions concern free homotopy classes of maps from S1S^1S1 and S2S^2S2. To convert a free homotopy to a based one, follow the moving image of the chosen domain point. Its path is precisely the shell change of basepoint. Thus representatives of two based classes are freely homotopic exactly when this action identifies them. For loops the action is conjugation, recovering Lecture 3’s statement: two loops based at x0x_0x0​ are freely homotopic exactly when their classes are conjugate. For spheres this is its higher-dimensional version (Mermin, §VII.D, p. 631). For the texture, adding the point at infinity compactifies the oriented plane to S2S^2S2. The fixed limiting value supplies its target basepoint. This gives the stated based class. 

If the value at infinity is also allowed to move, the texture likewise needs action orbits. A nonzero linking class obstructs extension across the core within M\mathcal MM: an extension to the transverse disk would contract the boundary map radially. A zero class admits such an extension because a representative map is null-homotopic. This is a topological obstruction, not a claim about the energy or time needed to remove the core. The local boundary classes do not classify knotting or all configurations with several defects.

Moving a linking circle through a region without cores preserves its free class. Indeed, composing the spatial deformation with the order-parameter map gives a homotopy. 26.7 Vortices, nematics and magnetic textures

The figure compares an oriented phase field with an unoriented director field on a linking circle.

Example 26.17 (Superfluid vortices). Determine the local line and point classes for a superfluid. A superfluid has a phase eiα∈S1e^{\ii\alpha}\in S^1eiα∈S1. On an oriented linking circle a vortex of winding n∈Zn\in\Zn∈Z is represented by ϕ↦einϕ\phi\mapsto e^{\ii n\phi}ϕ↦einϕ for 0≤ϕ≤2π0\le\phi\le2\pi0≤ϕ≤2π. Lecture 3 gives π1(S1)=Z\pi_1(S^1)=\Zπ1​(S1)=Z. This group is abelian, so its conjugacy classes are its individual integers. Based joining adds windings. Thus the enclosing winding of two vortices, joined as in the whiskered-loop figure, is the sum of their individual windings. Two vortices of winding +1+1+1 have total winding 222 and cannot cancel within S1S^1S1. Opposite windings have total zero and have no such obstruction. The group π2(S1)\pi_2(S^1)π2​(S1) vanishes, so there is no corresponding point charge.

For a neutral superfluid the phase also determines the circulation.

Definition 26.18 (Disclination). The nontrivial line defect in a uniaxial nematic is called a disclination.

Example 26.19 (Nematic lines and point charges). Determine the local line and point classes for a uniaxial nematic. A uniaxial nematic has M=RP2\mathcal M=\RP2M=RP2. Self-check.

An ordered medium has M=SO(3)\mathcal M=\SO(3)M=SO(3). Choose the linking domain in three-dimensional space. Find its local line classes and the result of joining two nontrivial lines.

Answer.

The linking domain is a circle transverse to the line. Lecture 4 gives π1(SO(3))=Z2\pi_1(\SO(3))=\Z_2π1​(SO(3))=Z2​. It is abelian, so there are two free line classes. The sum of two nontrivial classes is zero, removing this local topological obstruction. This does not assert that a particular physical evolution makes the defects annihilate.

Self-check.

A nematic configuration has two point defects. Chosen comparison paths give lifted degrees 333 and −2-2−2 at a common basepoint. Find the total based charge and its free label. Repeat when only the second path is changed by the nontrivial director loop. Explain why the local labels 333 and 222 do not determine a total free label.

Answer.

The first total is 3+(−2)=13+(-2)=13+(−2)=1, with free label 111. The changed path acts by the target antipodal map, so the second representative becomes +2+2+2. The new total is 3+2=53+2=53+2=5, with free label 555. The same local absolute values have lost the comparison-path information.

Summary

The groups πn\pi_nπn​ classify based sphere maps and are abelian for n≥2n\ge2n≥2. The cited sphere results and fibration sequence compute the groups used here. Local free defect classes still require conjugacy for loops and the fundamental-group action for spheres. Superfluid vortices retain signed integer winding. Nematic disclinations have Z2\Z_2Z2​ charge. Free nematic point charges identify nnn with −n-n−n. Sphere-valued hedgehogs and based plane textures have signed degree. Lecture 27 adds boundary conditions and energy to this topological classification.
