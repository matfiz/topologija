Lecture 26

Higher homotopy groups and topological defects

What this lecture does

A loop detects whether a line defect can be removed while the surrounding medium stays ordered. A sphere provides the corresponding test for a point defect. We extend the fundamental group to maps from higher-dimensional spheres. We then distinguish based charges from the free homotopy classes that describe local defects. This distinction identifies opposite point charges for unoriented axes, but preserves their sign for oriented magnetic directions.

26.2 Sphere groups and the Hopf map

The degree from Lecture 14 is invariant under homotopy. The converse for sphere maps is an additional theorem: equal degrees imply based homotopy. We cite it here rather than infer it from the integral formula alone.

Theorem 26.2 (Homotopy groups of spherescited). For every n≥1n\ge1n≥1, the degree map gives πn(Sn)≅Z\pi_n(S^n)\cong\Zπn​(Sn)≅Z. For 1≤k<n1\le k<n1≤k<n, the group πk(Sn)\pi_k(S^n)πk​(Sn) vanishes. The group π3(S2)\pi_3(S^2)π3​(S2) is infinite cyclic and is generated by the Hopf map S3→S2S^3\to S^2S3→S2 of Lecture 20.

The precise sources are Hatcher, Corollary 4.9, p. 349, Corollary 4.25, p. 361, and Example 4.51, p. 380. Hatcher’s homological degree is the integer multiplier ddd on Hn(Sn;Z)≅ZH_n(S^n;\Z)\cong\ZHn​(Sn;Z)≅Z. This definition is given in §2.2, p. 134. This multiplier does not depend on a choice of generator. For smooth maps, Lecture 14’s cited de Rham identification intertwines this action with pullback of forms: integrating a form over a composed simplex equals integrating its pullback over that simplex. Thus the homological degree agrees with the integral degree of Theorems 14.2 and 14.6. To verify this, let ccc be the period of a normalized volume form ν\nuν on a generator [S][S][S]. Its de Rham class is nonzero since ∫Snν=1\int_{S^n}\nu=1∫Sn​ν=1, so injectivity of the pairing gives c≠0c\ne0c=0. The top-cohomology argument in Lecture 14 gives [f∗ν]=(deg⁡f)[ν][f^*\nu]=(\mapdegree f)[\nu][f∗ν]=(degf)[ν]. Naturality and the vanishing of exact-form periods therefore give dc=∫f∗[S]ν=∫[S]f∗ν=(deg⁡f)c.d c=\int_{f_*[S]}\nu=\int_{[S]}f^*\nu=(\mapdegree f)c.dc=∫f∗​[S]​ν=∫[S]​f∗ν=(degf)c. Cancellation of ccc identifies the two degrees. For continuous maps, Hatcher’s definition uses homology directly. Thus the identity sphere map has charge +1+1+1 and generates πn(Sn)\pi_n(S^n)πn​(Sn). Maps from a smaller-dimensional sphere cannot carry such a charge. The Hopf result also shows that higher homotopy groups need not vanish when the domain sphere is larger.

We will also use the covering-space result in Hatcher, Proposition 4.1, p. 342: a covering induces an isomorphism on πn\pi_nπn​ for n≥2n\ge2n≥2. Sphere maps and their homotopies lift, which explains this result. The contractible cover R→S1\R\to S^1R→S1 gives πn(S1)=0\pi_n(S^1)=0πn​(S1)=0 for n≥2n\ge2n≥2. The cover S2→RP2S^2\to\RP2S2→RP2 gives π2(RP2)≅Z\pi_2(\RP2)\cong\Zπ2​(RP2)≅Z after choosing a lifted basepoint. That choice will matter when the basepoint is allowed to move.

26.3 The fibration sequence and two consequences

A fibration is a continuous map p ⁣:E→Bp\colon E\to Bp:E→B with the homotopy lifting property: for every space YYY, a homotopy Y×[0,1]→BY\times[0,1]\to BY×[0,1]→B with a chosen lift at its initial time has a continuous lift throughout that restricts to the chosen lift at time zero. For the theorem below it suffices to require this property for disks of every dimension. Fibre bundles have this property for disks by Hatcher’s Proposition 4.48, p. 379. Choose a basepoint b0∈Bb_0\in Bb0​∈B and a point e0e_0e0​ in its fibre F=p−1(b0)F=p^{-1}(b_0)F=p−1(b0​). All homotopy groups below use these compatible basepoints.

Theorem 26.3 (The fibration sequencecited). For a fibration over a path-connected base, the homotopy groups have a long exact sequence. Its relevant segment is ⋯⟶π2(F)⟶π2(E)⟶π2(B)→∂fibπ1(F)⟶π1(E)⟶π1(B)⟶⋯ .\cdots\longrightarrow\pi_2(F)\longrightarrow\pi_2(E) \longrightarrow\pi_2(B)\xrightarrow{\partial_{\mathrm{fib}}}\pi_1(F) \longrightarrow\pi_1(E)\longrightarrow\pi_1(B)\longrightarrow\cdots.⋯⟶π2​(F)⟶π2​(E)⟶π2​(B)∂fib​​π1​(F)⟶π1​(E)⟶π1​(B)⟶⋯. Exactness means that the image of each arrow is the kernel of the next.

This is Hatcher’s Theorem 4.41, p. 376. The symbol ∂fib\partial_{\mathrm{fib}}∂fib​ names the connecting homomorphism, distinct from a geometric boundary. Represent a based sphere by a square with its boundary collapsed. Lifting this square leaves a boundary loop in the fibre. Its construction and the general exactness proof are cited.

For the Hopf bundle, F=S1F=S^1F=S1, E=S3E=S^3E=S3 and B=S2B=S^2B=S2. The cited sphere groups give π2(S3)=0\pi_2(S^3)=0π2​(S3)=0 and π1(S3)=0\pi_1(S^3)=0π1​(S3)=0. The figure isolates the resulting segment.

Hence π2(S2)≅π1(S1)≅Z\pi_2(S^2)\cong\pi_1(S^1)\cong\Zπ2​(S2)≅π1​(S1)≅Z. This is a consequence of the cited sequence, not a second proof of that sequence.

More generally, let GGG be a simply connected Lie group with π2(G)=0\pi_2(G)=0π2​(G)=0 and let HHH be a closed Lie subgroup. Lecture 20 gives the principal bundle H→G→G/HH\to G\to G/HH→G→G/H. Exactness gives π2(G/H)≅π1(H)\pi_2(G/H)\cong\pi_1(H)π2​(G/H)≅π1​(H) because both adjacent groups from GGG vanish. Here the basepoint of HHH is its identity, so its loops lie in its identity component. The assumption π2(G)=0\pi_2(G)=0π2​(G)=0 is explicit. Simple connectedness by itself is not this assumption.

26.4 Order parameters and moving basepoints

An order parameter records the local ordered state of a medium. Its allowed ordered values form the order-parameter space M\mathcal MM. When a symmetry group GGG acts transitively on the uniform ordered states, Lecture 8 identifies this space with G/HG/HG/H, where HHH fixes a reference state. The relevant descriptions in this lecture are a superfluid phase in S1S^1S1, a unit ferromagnetic direction in S2S^2S2, and a nematic director in RP2\RP2RP2. A director is an unoriented unit axis: n\mathbf nn and −n-\mathbf n−n describe the same state. The word texture means a continuous spatial configuration of the order parameter.

For an ordinary based charge, a chosen point of the domain sphere must have a prescribed image x0x_0x0​. A deformation of the physical texture can move that image. We therefore need the effect of moving the basepoint. Let γ ⁣:[0,1]→M\gamma\colon[0,1]\to\mathcal Mγ:[0,1]→M run from x0x_0x0​ to x1x_1x1​. Rescale the domain of a cube map based at x1x_1x1​ onto a smaller concentric cube. On each radial segment of the surrounding shell, insert γ\gammaγ, running from x0x_0x0​ at the outer boundary to x1x_1x1​ at the inner boundary. The figure depicts this construction.

Proposition 26.4 (The basepoint actionsketched). The shell construction gives an isomorphism βγ ⁣:πn(M,x1)→πn(M,x0)\beta_\gamma\colon\pi_n(\mathcal M,x_1)\to\pi_n(\mathcal M,x_0)βγ​:πn​(M,x1​)→πn​(M,x0​). For loops at x0x_0x0​, it gives an action of π1(M,x0)\pi_1(\mathcal M,x_0)π1​(M,x0​) on πn(M,x0)\pi_n(\mathcal M,x_0)πn​(M,x0​). For n=1n=1n=1 this is conjugation.

Sketch. A homotopy of the path or inner map gives a homotopy of the shell map. Reversing the path gives the inverse isomorphism, since the retraced shell contracts. Two nested shells concatenate their paths. Consequently βγη=βγβη\beta_{\gamma\eta}=\beta_\gamma\beta_\etaβγη​=βγ​βη​ in the first-path-first convention. Joining inner cube maps shows that the induced maps preserve the group operation. For n=1n=1n=1 the shell map is the path γfγ−1\gamma f\gamma^{-1}γfγ−1, as in Lecture 3. These constructions give the asserted action. They are described in Hatcher, pp. 341–342. 

Even though πn\pi_nπn​ is abelian for n≥2n\ge2n≥2, this external action can be nontrivial. Commutativity of its own group law does not remove the basepoint issue.

26.5 Local defects and linking spheres

A defect core is a region where the chosen ordered description fails. Outside the cores the order parameter is a continuous map into M\mathcal MM. A small circle surrounding a line in three-dimensional space is a linking circle. A small sphere surrounding an isolated point is its linking sphere. The figure separates these two domains of the order-parameter map.

For a core of codimension ccc, the local transverse boundary is Sc−1S^{c-1}Sc−1. This explains which homotopy group occurs. The physical sphere’s orientation is fixed. Reversing it can reverse an integer charge. A topological defect has a nontrivial linking homotopy class that obstructs removal of its core within M\mathcal MM.

Theorem 26.5 (Local defect classessketched). Let M\mathcal MM be a path-connected order-parameter manifold. Classify restrictions to a linking domain by continuous homotopy through maps into M\mathcal MM. Without a fixed image basepoint, line defects in three dimensions have the conjugacy classes of π1(M)\pi_1(\mathcal M)π1​(M), and point defects have the orbits of π2(M)\pi_2(\mathcal M)π2​(M) under the π1(M)\pi_1(\mathcal M)π1​(M) action. A two-dimensional texture with a fixed uniform value at spatial infinity has a based class in π2(M)\pi_2(\mathcal M)π2​(M).

Sketch. The first assertions concern free homotopy classes of maps from S1S^1S1 and S2S^2S2. To convert a free homotopy to a based one, follow the moving image of the chosen domain point. Its path is precisely the shell change of basepoint. Thus representatives of two based classes are freely homotopic exactly when this action identifies them. For loops the action is conjugation, recovering Lecture 3’s full loop result. For spheres this is its higher-dimensional version (Mermin, §VII.D, p. 631). For the texture, adding the point at infinity compactifies the oriented plane to S2S^2S2. The fixed limiting value supplies its target basepoint. This gives the stated based class. 

If the value at infinity is also allowed to move, the texture likewise needs action orbits. A nonzero linking class obstructs extension across the core within M\mathcal MM: an extension to the transverse disk would contract the boundary map radially. A zero class admits such an extension because a representative map is null-homotopic. This is a topological obstruction, not a claim about the energy or time needed to remove the core. The local boundary classes do not classify knotting or all configurations with several defects.

Self-check.

An ordered medium has M=SO(3)\mathcal M=\SO(3)M=SO(3). Find its local line classes and the result of joining two nontrivial lines.

Answer.

Lecture 4 gives π1(SO(3))=Z2\pi_1(\SO(3))=\Z_2π1​(SO(3))=Z2​. It is abelian, so there are two free line classes. The sum of two nontrivial classes is zero, removing this local topological obstruction. This does not assert that a particular physical evolution makes the defects annihilate.

Summary

The groups πn\pi_nπn​ classify based sphere maps and are abelian for n≥2n\ge2n≥2. The cited sphere results and fibration sequence compute the groups used here. Local free defect classes still require conjugacy for loops and the fundamental-group action for spheres. Superfluid vortices retain signed integer winding. Nematic disclinations have Z2\Z_2Z2​ charge. Free nematic point charges identify nnn with −n-n−n. Sphere-valued hedgehogs and based plane textures have signed degree. Lecture 27 adds boundary conditions and energy to this topological classification.
