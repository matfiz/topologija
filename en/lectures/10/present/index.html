Lecture 10

Differential forms

A differential form assigns an alternating multilinear measurement to tangent vectors at each point. Forms pull back under every smooth map. Their exterior derivative extends the differential of a function and combines the Cartesian gradient, curl and divergence. We construct this derivative in coordinates, prove that it is independent of coordinates, and compute the sphere’s area form.

10.2 Alternating tensors and the wedge product

Self-check.

Compute d(xdy∧dz)\dd(x\dd y\wedge\dd z)d(xdy∧dz) on R3\R^3R3 and its pullback to S2\sphere2S2.

Answer.

The derivative is dx∧dy∧dz\dd x\wedge\dd y\wedge\dd zdx∧dy∧dz. Its pullback is zero: an alternating trilinear form vanishes on the two-dimensional tangent space of the sphere.

Summary

Forms alternate in their tangent arguments and use the determinant wedge. The exterior derivative is independent of coordinates, squares to zero and satisfies the graded product rule. Pullback exists under every smooth map and commutes with this derivative. A closed form need not be exact, as the angular form’s nonzero loop integral proves. The sphere’s outward area has negative coefficient in the north-pole stereographic coordinates. Lecture 11 differentiates forms along flows.
