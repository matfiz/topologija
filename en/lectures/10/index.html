Lecture 10

Differential forms

What this lecture does

A differential form supplies an integrand for curves, surfaces and higher-dimensional pieces of a manifold. At each point it assigns a signed multilinear measurement to tangent vectors. Forms pull back under every smooth map. Their exterior derivative extends the differential of a function and combines the Cartesian gradient, curl and divergence. We construct this derivative in coordinates, prove that it is independent of coordinates, and compute the sphere’s area form.

10.2 Alternating tensors and the wedge product

A signed surface integrand measures a parallelogram in a tangent space. Linearity expresses addition and scaling in each edge. Equal edges span no area, so the value is zero. For a bilinear map, expansion on (v+w,v+w)(v+w,v+w)(v+w,v+w) then shows that exchanging the edges reverses the sign. Alternating forms extend this rule to higher dimensions.

Definition 10.5 (Tensor field). A tensor field of type (r,s)(r,s)(r,s) assigns at each point a multilinear real-valued map with rrr cotangent arguments and sss tangent arguments. Its coordinate coefficients must be smooth. A tensor with r=0r=0r=0 is covariant.

Definition 10.6 (Differential form). A kkk-form is a covariant kkk-tensor field that is alternating: interchanging two arguments reverses its sign. We write Ωk(M)\forms{k}(M)Ωk(M) for the vector space of smooth kkk-forms. A 000-form is a smooth function.

The degree kkk counts tangent arguments, whereas nnn is the dimension of MMM. Alternation implies that every kkk-form is zero if k>dim⁡Mk>\dim Mk>dimM.

Self-check.

State in one sentence what a differential kkk-form is and why it is introduced. Explain why repeated tangent arguments give zero.

Answer.

A differential kkk-form is a smooth alternating multilinear measurement of kkk tangent vectors at each point, introduced as an integrand for signed kkk-dimensional integration. Exchanging two equal arguments leaves the value unchanged and negates it, so the value is zero.

Summary

Forms alternate in their tangent arguments and use the determinant wedge. The exterior derivative is independent of coordinates, squares to zero and satisfies the graded product rule. Pullback exists under every smooth map and commutes with this derivative. A closed form need not be exact, as the angular form’s nonzero loop integral proves. The sphere’s outward area has negative coefficient in the north-pole stereographic coordinates. Lecture 11 differentiates forms along flows.
