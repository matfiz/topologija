Lecture 2

Compactness, connectedness and gluing

Open sets let us define properties that distinguish spaces. Compactness expresses a finiteness condition on open covers. Connectedness rules out a separation into two open pieces. We prove how continuous maps preserve these properties, then use them to identify spaces formed by gluing. The main criterion turns a continuous bijection into a homeomorphism when its domain is compact and its target is Hausdorff.

2.1 Compactness

Definition 2.1 (Compactness). An open cover of a space XXX is a family of open sets (Uα)α∈I(U_\alpha)_{\alpha\in I}(Uα​)α∈I​ whose union is XXX. A subcover is a subfamily that still covers XXX. The space is compact if every open cover has a finite subcover.

A subset K⊆XK\subseteq XK⊆X is called compact when its subspace topology is compact. Equivalently, every cover of KKK by open sets of XXX has a finite subcover. This follows because the open sets of KKK are intersections K∩UK\cap UK∩U with ambient open sets UUU (§1.4).

The figure illustrates the condition on an interval. A finite selection must cover every point, including both endpoints.

Every finite space is compact. Choose one cover member containing each point. The resulting selection is finite. The empty space needs no members. In contrast, the real line is not compact. The open cover {(−n,n):n≥1}\{(-n,n):n\ge1\}{(−n,n):n≥1} has no finite subcover, since a finite selection is contained in its member with the largest index.

Theorem 2.2 (Continuous images and closed subsets). A continuous image of a compact space is compact. A closed subset of a compact space is compact.

Proof. Let f ⁣:X→Yf\colon X\to Yf:X→Y be continuous with XXX compact. Take an ambient open cover (Vα)(V_\alpha)(Vα​) of f(X)f(X)f(X). Its inverse images cover XXX and are open. Choose finitely many of them covering XXX. The corresponding sets VαV_\alphaVα​ cover f(X)f(X)f(X).

Now let C⊆XC\subseteq XC⊆X be closed, with XXX compact. Take an ambient open cover of CCC and add the open set X∖CX\setminus CX∖C. This covers XXX and therefore has a finite subcover. Discarding X∖CX\setminus CX∖C, if selected, leaves finitely many members covering CCC. 

We use two results about metric spaces with their proofs cited. Heine–Borel says that a subset of Rn\R^nRn is compact exactly when it is closed and bounded (Munkres, Theorem 27.3). Bounded means contained in some ball of finite radius. Thus closed intervals, closed balls and unit spheres are compact. The hypotheses refer to Euclidean space. Closed and bounded need not imply compact in a general metric space.

A metric space is sequentially compact if every sequence in it has a subsequence converging to a point of that space. This is the notion used in Linear Algebra for Physics, Lecture 13. It is equivalent to open-cover compactness for metric spaces (Munkres, Theorem 28.2). We use the open-cover definition on spaces without a specified metric.

The circle is compact by Heine–Borel, whereas the real line is not. They cannot be homeomorphic. A homeomorphism would take the compact circle onto the line. This settles one distinction promised in Lecture 1.

2.2 Compact spaces and Hausdorff targets

Hausdorffness means that distinct points have disjoint open neighbourhoods (§1.5). It supplies the other hypothesis in the homeomorphism criterion.

Proposition 2.3 (Compact subsets are closed). A compact subset of a Hausdorff space is closed.

Proof. Let K⊆YK\subseteq YK⊆Y be compact and y∈Y∖Ky\in Y\setminus Ky∈Y∖K. For each x∈Kx\in Kx∈K, choose disjoint open neighbourhoods UxU_xUx​ of xxx and VxV_xVx​ of yyy. Finitely many sets Ux1,…,UxmU_{x_1},\ldots,U_{x_m}Ux1​​,…,Uxm​​ cover KKK. The open neighbourhood Vx1∩⋯∩VxmV_{x_1}\cap\cdots\cap V_{x_m}Vx1​​∩⋯∩Vxm​​ of yyy misses their union, so misses KKK. Thus Y∖KY\setminus KY∖K is open. If KKK is empty, it is closed directly. 

Continuity can also be tested on closed sets. Inverse images commute with complements, so the open-set test is equivalent to the closed-set test.

Corollary 2.4 (Compact-to-Hausdorff criterion). A continuous bijection from a compact space to a Hausdorff space is a homeomorphism.

Proof. Let f ⁣:X→Yf\colon X\to Yf:X→Y satisfy the hypotheses. For a closed subset C⊆XC\subseteq XC⊆X, compactness of XXX makes CCC compact. Its image f(C)f(C)f(C) is compact, hence closed in YYY. This image is the inverse image of CCC under f−1 ⁣:Y→Xf^{-1}\colon Y\to Xf−1:Y→X. The closed-set test proves continuity of the inverse. 

Lecture 1’s continuous bijection [0,2π)→S1[0,2\pi)\to\sphere1[0,2π)→S1 does not meet the criterion. Its domain is not compact, since it is not closed in R\RR. We will instead use [0,1][0,1][0,1] and identify its two endpoints.

2.3 Connectedness

Definition 2.5 (Connectedness and paths). A separation of a space XXX is a pair of disjoint, nonempty open sets whose union is XXX. The space is connected if it has no separation. A path from xxx to yyy in XXX is a continuous map γ ⁣:[0,1]→X\gamma\colon[0,1]\to Xγ:[0,1]→X with γ(0)=x\gamma(0)=xγ(0)=x and γ(1)=y\gamma(1)=yγ(1)=y. The space is path-connected if such a path exists for every pair x,y∈Xx,y\in Xx,y∈X.

A subset is connected or path-connected when its subspace topology has the corresponding property.

Theorem 2.6 (Intervals, images and paths). The interval [0,1][0,1][0,1] is connected. Continuous images preserve connectedness and path-connectedness. Every path-connected space is connected.

Proof. Suppose [0,1]=U∪V[0,1]=U\cup V[0,1]=U∪V is a separation. Choose a∈Ua\in Ua∈U and b∈Vb\in Vb∈V, with a<ba<ba<b after exchanging the names if necessary. Put c=sup⁡(U∩[a,b])c=\sup(U\cap[a,b])c=sup(U∩[a,b]). If c∈Uc\in Uc∈U, then c<bc<bc<b, and openness of UUU gives a point of U∩[a,b]U\cap[a,b]U∩[a,b] larger than ccc. If c∈Vc\in Vc∈V, then c>ac>ac>a, and openness of VVV forbids points of UUU immediately below ccc. This contradicts the defining property of the supremum. Both cases are impossible, so no separation exists.

For a continuous map f ⁣:X→Yf\colon X\to Yf:X→Y, a separation of f(X)f(X)f(X) pulls back to a separation of XXX. Thus a connected domain has connected image. If XXX is path-connected, composing its paths with fff gives paths between any two image points.

Finally, a path-connected space cannot have a separation U∪VU\cup VU∪V. A path joining a point of UUU to a point of VVV would have connected image, since [0,1][0,1][0,1] is connected. Its intersections with UUU and VVV would separate that image. 

For example, a square and a ball are path-connected. The segment γ(t)=(1−t)x+ty\gamma(t)=(1-t)x+tyγ(t)=(1−t)x+ty stays in the set and joins any two of its points.

A connected component is a connected subset maximal under inclusion. The component of x∈Xx\in Xx∈X is the union of all connected subsets containing xxx. This union is connected. In any proposed separation, each of its connected subsets must lie wholly on the side containing xxx. It is maximal by its definition. Two components that meet therefore coincide, so components partition XXX.

Removing a point distinguishes a line from a plane, as the figure shows.

The space R∖{a}\R\setminus\{a\}R∖{a} has a separation into its two open rays. In Rn∖{p}\R^n\setminus\{p\}Rn∖{p}, with n≥2n\ge2n≥2, take points xxx and yyy and choose zzz outside the two lines through p,xp,xp,x and through p,yp,yp,y. Each affine formula is continuous, so the preimage of a closed set is closed on its half-interval. It is also closed in [0,1][0,1][0,1], since both half-intervals are closed there. Their finite union is closed, proving continuity of γ\gammaγ. Thus the punctured space is path-connected.

A homeomorphism restricts to a homeomorphism after deleting a point and its image. The punctured line and plane have different connectedness properties, so R≇R2\R\not\cong\R^2R≅R2. The radial map x↦x/∥x∥x\mapsto x/\norm xx↦x/∥x∥ also shows that Sn\sphere nSn is path-connected for n≥1n\ge1n≥1, as an image of Rn+1∖{0}\R^{n+1}\setminus\{0\}Rn+1∖{0}.

The converse to path-connected implies connected is false. A cited example is the closure of {(x,sin⁡(1/x)):0<x≤1}\{(x,\sin(1/x)):0<x\le1\}{(x,sin(1/x)):0<x≤1} in R2\R^2R2, the topologist’s sine curve (Munkres, §24, Example 7). Its analysis is reserved for Sheet 2.

2.4 Quotients and orbit spaces

Definition 2.7 (Quotient topology). For an equivalence relation ∼\sim∼ on XXX, the quotient set X/∼X/{\sim}X/∼ consists of the equivalence classes. The projection q ⁣:X→X/∼q\colon X\to X/{\sim}q:X→X/∼ sends each point to its class. The quotient topology is defined by V⊆X/∼ is open⟺q−1(V) is open in X.V\subseteq X/{\sim}\text{ is open} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad q^{-1}(V)\text{ is open in }X.V⊆X/∼ is open⟺q−1(V) is open in X.

Inverse images preserve unions and finite intersections, so this is a topology. The projection is continuous and surjective. Hence a quotient of a compact or path-connected space retains that property.

Proof. Composition with continuous qqq preserves continuity. A fibre is the inverse image of one point. This gives a general method: construct a continuous map with exactly the desired classes as its fibres, then apply the criterion.

Recall that a group has an associative product, an identity eee and inverses. An action by homeomorphisms of a group GGG on XXX assigns a homeomorphism x↦gxx\mapsto gxx↦gx to each g∈Gg\in Gg∈G, with ex=xex=xex=x and (gh)x=g(hx)(gh)x=g(hx)(gh)x=g(hx). For n≥1n\ge1n≥1, the real projective space RPn\RP nRPn is Sn/(x∼−x)\sphere n/(x\sim -x)Sn/(x∼−x). Its points are the lines through the origin in Rn+1\R^{n+1}Rn+1. The Hausdorff conclusion fails for general orbit spaces. Let the additive group Q\QQ act on R\RR by translation. A nonempty open union UUU of orbits contains an interval. Hence the only open sets of R/Q\R/\QR/Q are the empty set and the whole space. There are distinct classes, for example those of 000 and 2\sqrt22​, so this quotient is not Hausdorff. The Hausdorff condition will matter for the spaces constructed in Lectures 7 and 8.

2.5 Rotations as a glued ball

Self-check.

Prove that S1\sphere1S1 and [0,1][0,1][0,1] are not homeomorphic by removing one point. Explain why deleting an endpoint of the interval is not a useful test.

Answer.

Delete the midpoint of [0,1][0,1][0,1]. The two resulting half-intervals give a separation. Deleting any point from the circle leaves a space homeomorphic to R\RR. Rotate that point to the north pole, then use Lecture 1’s stereographic projection. The line is path-connected by straight segments. A homeomorphism would restrict to a homeomorphism between these two punctured spaces, which is impossible. Deleting an endpoint leaves a path-connected half-open interval, so connectedness would not distinguish them.

Summary

Continuous images preserve compactness and connectedness. A continuous bijection from compact to Hausdorff is a homeomorphism. A quotient topology tests continuity after composition with its projection. Quotients preserve compactness and path-connectedness, but Hausdorffness needs its own proof. The rotation group is the closed three-ball with antipodal boundary points identified, hence RP3\RP3RP3. Lecture 3 begins the study of loops.
