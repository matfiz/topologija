Lecture 20

Fibre bundles

What this lecture does

A vector field assigns a vector in a different tangent space at each point. A bundle describes these fibres and the changes between their local coordinates. We reconstruct bundles from transition functions and prove the global-section criterion for principal bundles. The Hopf bundle and the tangent sphere show how local products can fail to give a global product.

Definition 20.7 (Local frame). A local frame of a rank-rrr vector bundle is a row of rrr smooth sections that form a basis in each fibre of its domain.

The frames of a single vector space have no preferred member. Given two row frames ppp and p′p'p′, exactly one invertible matrix aaa satisfies p′=pap'=pap′=pa. Collecting the frames of all fibres gives a space over MMM. Its points are bases. Right multiplication changes those bases. A principal bundle records this space of choices and the group relating them.

Definition 20.8 (Principal bundle). Let G\LiegroupG be a Lie group. A principal G\LiegroupG-bundle is a bundle πE ⁣:P→M\bundleprojection\colon P\to MπE​:P→M with a smooth right action of G\LiegroupG that is free and transitive on each fibre. Freeness and transitivity say that any two points of a fibre differ by exactly one group element. Choosing φi(x,e)\varphi_i(x,\groupidentity)φi​(x,e) chooses a reference in that fibre. The group identity belongs to this coordinate description. It is not a preferred point of the fibre. For the frame bundle, the reference is a basis. For the Hopf bundle, it will be a unit representative of a complex line.

Lecture 4’s smooth covering examples are bundles with discrete fibre. The sheets over an evenly covered set give the local product. The antipodal cover Sn→RPn\sphere n\to\RP nSn→RPn is a principal Z2\Z_2Z2​-bundle under the action x↦±xx\mapsto\pm xx↦±x. The rotation cover S3→SO(3)\sphere3\to\SO(3)S3→SO(3) has the same property. A general covering need not have a deck group transitive on its fibres.

20.3 Sections and frames of a vector bundle

In a local frame, write s=eiσis=\tangentframe_i\sectioncoeff_is=ei​σi​. Its coefficient columns satisfy σi=gijσj.\sectioncoeff_i=\transition_{ij}\sectioncoeff_j.σi​=gij​σj​. Conversely, smooth columns with this compatibility glue to a section. The zero columns always give a global zero section.

A global frame exists exactly when the vector bundle is trivial. Indeed, a frame defines the isomorphism (x,v)↦e(x)v(x,v)\mapsto\tangentframe(x)v(x,v)↦e(x)v. Its inverse is smooth in local coordinates because the frame matrix is invertible. A trivialization supplies the converse frame by its standard basis. For a line bundle, one nowhere-zero section is a frame. For higher rank, one section need not give an entire basis.

In the Möbius line bundle, the intermediate value argument excludes a nowhere-zero section, so no global frame exists. The zero section gives no basis of a fibre.

A nowhere-zero section would have a continuous coefficient satisfying f(2π)=−f(0)f(2\pi)=-f(0)f(2π)=−f(0), which the intermediate value theorem excludes. A trivial line bundle has a nowhere-zero section, so the bundle is nontrivial. Its zero section still exists. For signs (−1,−1)(-1,-1)(−1,−1), change the frame on U2U_2U2​ by −1-1−1. The change law gives 1−1(−1)(−1)=+11^{-1}(-1)(-1)=+11−1(−1)(−1)=+1 on both components. These are the cylinder transitions, so this bundle is trivial.

Self-check.

State in one sentence what a fibre bundle is and why we introduced it when studying vector fields. Explain what local product charts do not determine by themselves.

Answer.

A fibre bundle is a smooth space over a base that is locally a product with a fixed fibre, introduced to describe the varying spaces in which fields take values. The overlap identifications determine how the products fit together. The cylinder and the Möbius line bundle have the same typical fibre R\RR but different overlap signs, so local product shape alone does not determine triviality.

The resulting polygon lies in finitely many two-dimensional linear subspaces. Their union misses a point of the sphere. Each gives a nonzero polynomial, so a parameter outside their finite root sets gives a vector outside the union. Normalize it to obtain the omitted sphere point. Stereographic projection from that point puts the polygon in Rn\R^nRn, where straight contraction to its base point fixes that base point. Pasting the piece homotopies proves the assertion for n=2n=2n=2 and n=3n=3n=3. Both spheres are path-connected by short arcs, joining antipodes via a third point.

The map [p,v]↦([p],vp)[p,v]\mapsto([p],vp)[p,v]↦([p],vp) identifies the associated line with T\mathcal TT. It is well-defined because (pu,v)∼(p,uv)(pu,v)\sim(p,uv)(pu,v)∼(p,uv). By the section criterion, Hopf nontriviality excludes a global smooth phase convention. A continuous global section would also give a homeomorphism with S2×S1\sphere2\times\sphere1S2×S1: in local coordinates the inverse uses multiplication by the inverse of the section’s continuous phase. We recall from Lecture 3 that winding classifies loops in U(1)\U(1)U(1) up to homotopy. For the bundle classification, we cite that bundles over either disk are trivial. Trivializing over the two caps leaves one transition on their overlap, which retracts onto the equator. Changing the section on a cap multiplies this transition by a phase that extends across a disk. Such a boundary phase has winding zero, since its loop contracts across that disk. Hence changing cap sections preserves the transition winding. The cited clutching theorem also says that homotopic equatorial transitions give isomorphic topological bundles. Together these facts explain why a single integer classifies them.

Proposition 20.22 (Clutching classificationcited). The underlying topological principal U(1)\U(1)U(1)-bundles over S2\sphere2S2, up to continuous equivariant bundle isomorphism, are classified by the winding of the transition gNS\transition_{NS}gNS​ on the positive equator. Every integer occurs.

Summary

Transition functions reconstruct smooth vector and principal bundles. Frames transform by ej=eigij\tangentframe_j=\tangentframe_i\transition_{ij}ej​=ei​gij​. The same transition gives σi=gijσj\sectioncoeff_i=\transition_{ij}\sectioncoeff_jσi​=gij​σj​. A principal bundle is trivial exactly when it has a global section. With chart order (N,S)=(z,w)(N,S)=(z,w)(N,S)=(z,w), the tangent sphere has transition winding +2+2+2. The inverse component map has winding −2-2−2.
