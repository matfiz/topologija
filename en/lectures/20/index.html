Lecture 20

Fibre bundles

What this lecture does

A vector field assigns a vector in a different tangent space at each point. A bundle describes these fibres and the changes between their local coordinates. We reconstruct bundles from transition functions and prove the global-section criterion for principal bundles. The Hopf bundle and the tangent sphere show how local products can fail to give a global product.

20.1 Local products and fibres

A smooth fibre bundle consists of a smooth surjection πE ⁣:E→M\bundleprojection\colon E\to MπE​:E→M and a manifold FFF, its typical fibre. Every point of the base space MMM has an open neighbourhood UiU_iUi​ and a diffeomorphism φi ⁣:Ui×F⟶πE−1(Ui),πE(φi(x,v))=x.\varphi_i\colon U_i\times F\longrightarrow\bundleprojection^{-1}(U_i), \qquad \bundleprojection(\varphi_i(x,v))=x.φi​:Ui​×F⟶πE−1​(Ui​),πE​(φi​(x,v))=x. This is a local trivialization. The manifold EEE is the total space. The fibre at xxx is Ex=πE−1(x)E_x=\bundleprojection^{-1}(x)Ex​=πE−1​(x). All manifolds here are Hausdorff and second countable. A bundle isomorphism over MMM is a diffeomorphism of total spaces commuting with the projections and preserving the specified fibre structure. A bundle is trivial if it is isomorphic to M×FM\times FM×F. The figure shows a local product only.

A real or complex vector bundle of rank rrr has vector-space fibres and local trivializations linear on each fibre, with typical fibre Vrep=Rr\repfibre=\R^rVrep​=Rr or Cr\C^rCr. A rank-one vector bundle is a line bundle. For example, TM\tangentbundle MTM of Lecture 8 is a rank-dim⁡M\dim MdimM real vector bundle.

A section over an open set U⊂MU\subset MU⊂M is a smooth map s ⁣:U→Es\colon U\to Es:U→E such that πE∘s=id⁡U\bundleprojection\circ s=\operatorname{id}_UπE​∘s=idU​. It is a global section when U=MU=MU=M. A local frame of a rank-rrr vector bundle is a row of rrr smooth sections that form a basis in each fibre of its domain.

Let G\LiegroupG be a Lie group. A principal G\LiegroupG-bundle is a bundle πE ⁣:P→M\bundleprojection\colon P\to MπE​:P→M with a smooth right action of G\LiegroupG that is free and transitive on each fibre. Its local trivializations are required to satisfy φi(x,ab)=φi(x,a)b\varphi_i(x,ab)=\varphi_i(x,a)bφi​(x,ab)=φi​(x,a)b.

The same construction with fibre a Lie group G\LiegroupG and transition functions acting by left multiplication constructs a principal bundle. Proof. Take the disjoint union of Ui×VrepU_i\times\repfibreUi​×Vrep​ and identify (x,v)j∼(x,gij(x)v)i.(x,v)_j\sim(x,\transition_{ij}(x)v)_i.(x,v)j​∼(x,gij​(x)v)i​. Identity, inverse and product in the cocycle make this an equivalence relation. Project its quotient to MMM by the common base point. Over UiU_iUi​, each class has a unique representative in the iiith copy. The corresponding local coordinate map sends a representative in copy jjj to (x,gij(x)v)(x,\transition_{ij}(x)v)(x,gij​(x)v). It is a homeomorphism because the preimage of an open set in one copy is open in every other copy, since the transition map is a diffeomorphism. The quotient topology therefore agrees with these product charts. Their changes are smooth and linear on fibres.

Points with different base points have disjoint neighbourhoods lifted from MMM. Points with the same base point can be separated in one product chart. Thus the total space is Hausdorff. Second countability of MMM gives a countable subcover. Choose a basis element inside a cover member at each point and choose one member per basis element. The corresponding product charts, each second countable, give a countable basis for the total space. Hence the quotient is a smooth manifold.

Finally, ej′=ei′hi−1gijhj\tangentframe_j'=\tangentframe_i'\framechange_i^{-1} \transition_{ij}\framechange_jej′​=ei′​hi−1​gij​hj​, proving the displayed change law. This is the smooth reconstruction of Nakahara, §9.2.2, with our frame convention. Naber’s Theorem 4.3.4, pp. 223–224, is its continuous principal-bundle counterpart, with the same transition convention.

20.3 Sections and frames of a vector bundle

In a local frame, write s=eiσis=\tangentframe_i\sectioncoeff_is=ei​σi​. Its coefficient columns satisfy σi=gijσj.\sectioncoeff_i=\transition_{ij}\sectioncoeff_j.σi​=gij​σj​. Conversely, smooth columns with this compatibility glue to a section. The zero columns always give a global zero section.

A global frame exists exactly when the vector bundle is trivial. Indeed, a frame defines the isomorphism (x,v)↦e(x)v(x,v)\mapsto\tangentframe(x)v(x,v)↦e(x)v. Its inverse is smooth in local coordinates because the frame matrix is invertible. A trivialization supplies the converse frame by its standard basis. For a line bundle, one nowhere-zero section is a frame. For higher rank, one section need not give an entire basis.

20.4 Principal and associated bundles

The local sections si(x)=φi(x,e)s_i(x)=\varphi_i(x,\groupidentity)si​(x)=φi​(x,e) obey sj=sigijs_j=s_i\transition_{ij}sj​=si​gij​. The group acts on the right, while gij\transition_{ij}gij​ changes fibre coordinates by multiplication on the left.

Proposition 20.2 (The section criterionproved). A principal bundle is trivial if and only if it has a global section.

Proof. A global section sss gives a map Ψ ⁣:M×G→P\Psi\colon M\times\Liegroup\to PΨ:M×G→P by Ψ(x,a)=s(x)a\Psi(x,a)=s(x)aΨ(x,a)=s(x)a. Freeness and transitivity make it bijective and equivariant. This is Nakahara, Theorem 9.2. Vector-bundle triviality requires a full frame, as above.

For an oriented Riemannian nnn-manifold, its oriented orthonormal frame bundle consists of all positive orthonormal frames in its tangent spaces. Right multiplication of a row frame by SO(n)\SO(n)SO(n) is free and transitive on each fibre. Local Gram–Schmidt frames give equivariant product charts. This makes it a principal SO(n)\SO(n)SO(n)-bundle, even when no global tangent frame exists.

To prove that SnS^nSn is simply connected for n∈{2,3}n\in\{2,3\}n∈{2,3}, subdivide a loop into hemispherical pieces using Lemma 3.5. On each piece replace it by the normalized straight segment between its endpoints. Both curves have positive scalar product with the hemisphere centre, so their normalized straight interpolation is a homotopy fixing those endpoints. The resulting polygon lies in finitely many two-dimensional linear subspaces. Their union misses a point of the sphere. For each subspace choose a nonzero linear functional vanishing on it and evaluate these on (1,t,…,tn)(1,t,\ldots,t^n)(1,t,…,tn). Each gives a nonzero polynomial, so a parameter outside their finite root sets gives a vector outside the union. Normalize it to obtain the omitted sphere point. Stereographic projection from that point puts the polygon in Rn\R^nRn, where straight contraction to its base point fixes that base point. Pasting the piece homotopies proves the assertion for n=2n=2n=2 and n=3n=3n=3. Both spheres are path-connected by short arcs, joining antipodes via a third point.

Self-check.

Describe the Möbius real line bundle over S1\sphere1S1 and show it is nontrivial.

Answer.

Take [0,2π]×R[0,2\pi]\times\R[0,2π]×R and identify (2π,v)(2\pi,v)(2π,v) with (0,−v)(0,-v)(0,−v). On two overlapping base arcs the transition is +1+1+1 on one overlap component and −1-1−1 on the other. A trivial line bundle has a nowhere-zero section, so this bundle is nontrivial. Its zero section still exists. Putting −1-1−1 on both overlap components instead gives a trivial bundle. Change the frame on one whole arc by −1-1−1 to make both transitions +1+1+1.

Summary

Transition functions reconstruct smooth vector and principal bundles. Frames transform by ej=eigij\tangentframe_j=\tangentframe_i\transition_{ij}ej​=ei​gij​, while section components transform inversely. A principal bundle is trivial exactly when it has a global section. The Hopf bundle S3→S2\sphere3\to\sphere2S3→S2 is nontrivial and its associated standard line is tautological. The tangent sphere has frame winding +2+2+2, opposite to its component winding. Lecture 21 describes connections and transport in these local frames.
