Lecture 13

De Rham cohomology

What this lecture does

A closed form of positive degree has local primitives, forms whose exterior derivative is the given form, but a global primitive may not exist. De Rham cohomology records this obstruction. We prove the local assertion by integrating along a contraction. We then compare homotopic maps and compute the cohomology of spheres by joining two open sets. The Coulomb form gives a nonzero class with a measurable period.

13.1 Closed forms modulo exact forms

All manifolds in this lecture are smooth and without boundary. All vector spaces and cohomology groups have real coefficients. Recall from Lecture 10 that a form α\alphaα is closed if dα=0\dd\alpha=0dα=0 and exact if α=dη\alpha=\dd\etaα=dη for some form η\etaη. The identity d2=0\dd^2=0d2=0 makes every exact form closed. A cochain complex is a sequence of vector spaces with linear maps d ⁣:Ck→Ck+1\dd\colon C^k\to C^{k+1}d:Ck→Ck+1 whose successive composites are zero. The spaces Ωk(M)\forms k(M)Ωk(M) with exterior differentiation form such a complex. We put Ωk(M)=0\forms k(M)=0Ωk(M)=0 for k<0k<0k<0.

The de Rham cohomology in degree kkk is the quotient Hk(M)=ker⁡(d ⁣:Ωk(M)→Ωk+1(M))im⁡(d ⁣:Ωk−1(M)→Ωk(M)).\cohomology k(M) =\frac{\ker(\dd\colon\forms k(M)\to\forms{k+1}(M))} {\operatorname{im}(\dd\colon\forms{k-1}(M)\to\forms k(M))}.Hk(M)=im(d:Ωk−1(M)→Ωk(M))ker(d:Ωk(M)→Ωk+1(M))​. The cohomology class [α][\alpha][α] of a closed form consists of all closed forms differing from α\alphaα by an exact form. In positive degree, [α]=0[\alpha]=0[α]=0 means precisely that α\alphaα has a global primitive. In degree zero, only the zero function represents the zero class. The superscript distinguishes cohomology from Lecture 6’s homology. The only exact form of degree zero is zero. A function is closed exactly when all its partial derivatives vanish. It is then constant on connected coordinate balls, hence locally constant. Conversely a locally constant function has zero derivative. In particular, H0(M)=R\cohomology0(M)=\RH0(M)=R when MMM is connected and nonempty.

For example, on the punctured plane the angular form is αang=−ydx+xdyx2+y2.\alpha_{\mathrm{ang}}=\frac{-y\dd x+x\dd y}{x^2+y^2}.αang​=x2+y2−ydx+xdy​. Lecture 10 proved that this form is closed and has integral 2π2\pi2π around the counter-clockwise unit circle. An exact form has zero integral around that circle by Stokes. Hence [αang]≠0[\alpha_{\mathrm{ang}}]\ne0[αang​]=0. More generally, a period of a closed kkk-form is its integral over a closed oriented kkk-manifold mapped smoothly into MMM, using pullback. Such a mapped manifold is a geometric cycle. Stokes makes every period of an exact form zero, so periods depend only on the class. These geometric cycles suffice here. Lecture 14 relates forms to the singular cycles of Lecture 6.

A smooth map f ⁣:M→Nf\colon M\to Nf:M→N induces a linear map f∗ ⁣:Hk(N)→Hk(M)f^*\colon\cohomology k(N)\to\cohomology k(M)f∗:Hk(N)→Hk(M) by f∗[α]=[f∗α]f^*[\alpha]=[f^*\alpha]f∗[α]=[f∗α]. Indeed, pullback commutes with d\ddd, so it preserves closed forms and exact differences. Pullback reverses composition: (g∘f)∗=f∗g∗(g\circ f)^*=f^*g^*(g∘f)∗=f∗g∗, and the identity map induces the identity. The wedge product also descends to classes. For closed β\betaβ, (dη)∧β=d(η∧β)(\dd\eta)\wedge\beta=\dd(\eta\wedge\beta)(dη)∧β=d(η∧β). For closed α\alphaα of degree kkk, α∧dξ=(−1)kd(α∧ξ)\alpha\wedge\dd\xi=(-1)^k\dd(\alpha\wedge\xi)α∧dξ=(−1)kd(α∧ξ). Changing either representative therefore changes the product only by an exact form. We write H(M)\cohomology*(M)H(M) for all the degrees together.

13.3 Homotopy invariance

Theorem 13.3 (Homotopy invarianceproved given cited smoothing; smooth homotopies proved directly). Smoothly homotopic maps induce the same maps on de Rham cohomology. The same holds for smooth maps that are continuously homotopic. Smooth manifolds with the same continuous homotopy type have isomorphic cohomology in every degree.

Proof. For a smooth homotopy and a closed form α\alphaα, the operator identity gives F1∗α−F0∗α=dKFαF_1^*\alpha-F_0^*\alpha=\dd K_F\alphaF1∗​α−F0∗​α=dKF​α. The two pullbacks therefore represent the same class.

For the continuous case we use the following smooth approximation results. Every continuous map between smooth manifolds is homotopic to a smooth map. Two homotopic smooth maps admit a smooth homotopy. These are cited from Lee, Introduction to Smooth Manifolds, second edition, Theorems 6.26 and 6.29, pp. 141–143. Choose a smooth representative of a continuous map’s homotopy class and use its pullback. Two choices induce the same map by the second cited result and the smooth proof above. Composition and identities are preserved because compositions of smooth representatives represent the continuous compositions. If continuous maps f ⁣:M→Nf\colon M\to Nf:M→N and g ⁣:N→Mg\colon N\to Mg:N→M are homotopy inverses, their composites represent identity maps. Contravariance then gives f∗g∗=id⁡f^*g^*=\idf∗g∗=id and g∗f∗=id⁡g^*f^*=\idg∗f∗=id on the respective cohomology spaces. Thus these maps are inverse isomorphisms. 

A contractible manifold consequently has the cohomology of a point: R\RR in degree zero and zero in positive degrees. For n≥1n\ge1n≥1, the punctured space Rn∖{0}\R^n\setminus\{0\}Rn∖{0} retracts onto Sn−1\sphere{n-1}Sn−1 by r(x)=x/∥x∥r(x)=x/\norm xr(x)=x/∥x∥. With iii the inclusion, r∘i=id⁡r\circ i=\idr∘i=id. A smooth homotopy from the identity to i∘ri\circ ri∘r is F(t,x)=((1−t)2+t2∥x∥)x.F(t,x)=\left((1-t)^2+\frac{t^2}{\norm x}\right)x.F(t,x)=((1−t)2+∥x∥t2​)x. The coefficient is positive for every real ttt, since its two nonnegative summands never vanish together. Hence the homotopy stays in the punctured space. It has the required endpoints. This proves H(Rn∖{0})≅H(Sn−1)\cohomology*(\R^n\setminus\{0\})\cong\cohomology*(\sphere{n-1})H(Rn∖{0})≅H(Sn−1).

Real coefficients lose torsion information. A torsion element is one annihilated by a positive integer. No real vector space has a nonzero torsion element. In particular, Lecture 4’s order-two loop in SO(3)\SO(3)SO(3) cannot be detected by a closed real 1-form’s period. Use smooth loops constant near their endpoints, so their concatenation is smooth. Periods of a closed 1-form are invariant under smooth loop homotopy by Stokes on the cylinder. Continuous homotopies can be smoothed by the cited theorem. Periods add under loop composition, so twice that loop’s period is zero and its real period is zero. This does not impose any integer normalization on the sphere class. Lecture 22 obtains that extra information from a bundle.

Self-check.

Find H1(R2∖{0})\cohomology1(\R^2\setminus\{0\})H1(R2∖{0}) and a generator of period one on the counter-clockwise unit circle.

Answer.

Radial retraction gives the cohomology of S1S^1S1, so this space is one-dimensional. The class [αang/(2π)][\alpha_{\mathrm{ang}}/(2\pi)][αang​/(2π)] has period one by Lecture 10. It is nonzero and therefore spans the space.

Summary

De Rham cohomology consists of closed forms modulo exact forms, over R\RR. The homotopy operator proves local exactness in positive degree on star-shaped sets and invariance under smooth homotopy. Cited smoothing extends invariance to continuous homotopy type. Mayer–Vietoris computes sphere cohomology from two contractible sets. On S2S^2S2, the area class [β/(4π)][\beta/(4\pi)][β/(4π)] has period one. Lecture 14 relates periods to degree and singular homology.
