Lecture 13

De Rham cohomology

A closed form of positive degree has local primitives, forms whose exterior derivative is the given form, but a global primitive may not exist. De Rham cohomology records this obstruction. We prove the local assertion by integrating along a contraction. We then compare homotopic maps and compute the cohomology of spheres by joining two open sets. The Coulomb form gives a nonzero class with a measurable period.

Unit 13A Local primitives and homotopy

13.1 Closed forms modulo exact forms

All manifolds in this lecture are smooth and without boundary. All vector spaces and cohomology groups have real coefficients. Recall from Lecture 10 that a form α\alphaα is closed if dα=0\dd\alpha=0dα=0 and exact if α=dη\alpha=\dd\etaα=dη for some form η\etaη. The identity d2=0\dd^2=0d2=0 makes every exact form closed.

Lecture 6 formed homology as the kernel of a boundary map modulo the image of the preceding boundary map. In degree zero there are no forms of degree −1-1−1, so the only exact form is zero. A function is closed exactly when all its partial derivatives vanish. It is then constant on connected coordinate balls, hence locally constant. Conversely a locally constant function has zero derivative. In particular, H0(M)=R\cohomology0(M)=\RH0(M)=R when MMM is connected and nonempty. For a disconnected manifold, each component contributes an independently chosen constant. 13.3 Homotopy invariance

Theorem 13.12 (Homotopy invarianceproved given cited smoothing; smooth homotopies proved directly). Smoothly homotopic maps induce the same maps on de Rham cohomology. The same holds for smooth maps that are continuously homotopic. Smooth manifolds with the same continuous homotopy type have isomorphic cohomology in every degree.

Proof. For a smooth homotopy and a closed form α\alphaα, the operator identity gives F1∗α−F0∗α=dKFαF_1^*\alpha-F_0^*\alpha=\dd K_F\alphaF1∗​α−F0∗​α=dKF​α. The two pullbacks therefore represent the same class.

For the continuous case we use the following smooth approximation results. Every continuous map between smooth manifolds is homotopic to a smooth map. Two homotopic smooth maps admit a smooth homotopy. These are cited from Lee, Introduction to Smooth Manifolds, second edition, Theorems 6.26 and 6.29, pp. 141–143. Choose a smooth representative of a continuous map’s homotopy class and use its pullback. Two choices induce the same map by the second cited result and the smooth proof above. Composition and identities are preserved because compositions of smooth representatives represent the continuous compositions. If continuous maps f ⁣:M→Nf\colon M\to Nf:M→N and g ⁣:N→Mg\colon N\to Mg:N→M are homotopy inverses, their composites represent identity maps. Contravariance then gives f∗g∗=id⁡f^*g^*=\idf∗g∗=id and g∗f∗=id⁡g^*f^*=\idg∗f∗=id on the respective cohomology spaces. Thus these maps are inverse isomorphisms. 

A contractible manifold consequently has the cohomology of a point: R\RR in degree zero and zero in positive degrees. For n≥1n\ge1n≥1, the punctured space Rn∖{0}\R^n\setminus\{0\}Rn∖{0} retracts onto Sn−1\sphere{n-1}Sn−1 by r(x)=x/∥x∥r(x)=x/\norm xr(x)=x/∥x∥. With iii the inclusion, r∘i=id⁡r\circ i=\idr∘i=id. A smooth homotopy from the identity to i∘ri\circ ri∘r is F(t,x)=((1−t)2+t2∥x∥)x.F(t,x)=\left((1-t)^2+\frac{t^2}{\norm x}\right)x.F(t,x)=((1−t)2+∥x∥t2​)x. We use squares in this coefficient to obtain a homotopy defined for all real ttt, including smooth extensions past both endpoints. The coefficient is positive for every real ttt, since its two nonnegative summands never vanish together. Hence the homotopy stays in the punctured space. It has the required endpoints. This proves H(Rn∖{0})≅H(Sn−1)\cohomology*(\R^n\setminus\{0\})\cong\cohomology*(\sphere{n-1})H(Rn∖{0})≅H(Sn−1).

Self-check.

State what a de Rham cohomology class is and why we introduced it. Explain how a nonzero period distinguishes a class from zero.

Answer.

A class is a closed form modulo addition of exact forms. In positive degree, the zero class consists of forms with a global primitive. We introduced the quotient to measure the obstruction to such a primitive. Stokes makes every period of an exact form zero, so a nonzero period proves that a closed form represents a nonzero class.

13.4 Exact sequences and Mayer–Vietoris

Lecture 5 used van Kampen to compute the fundamental group of a union from two open sets and their overlap. Mayer–Vietoris uses the same cover to compute cohomology. We restrict forms to the pieces, compare them on the overlap, and differentiate local lifts to recover a global class.

Definition 13.13. A sequence of vector spaces and linear maps is exact at a term if the image of the incoming map equals the kernel of the outgoing map.

Definition 13.14. A short exact sequence has the form 0→A→B→C→00\to A\to B\to C\to00→A→B→C→0.

The uses are related: a cochain complex is exact at a degree precisely when every closed element in that degree is exact.

Summary

De Rham cohomology consists of closed forms modulo exact forms, over R\RR. The homotopy operator proves local exactness in positive degree on star-shaped sets and invariance under smooth homotopy. Cited smoothing extends invariance to continuous homotopy type. Mayer–Vietoris computes sphere cohomology from two contractible sets. On S2S^2S2, the area class [β/(4π)][\beta/(4\pi)][β/(4π)] has period one. Lecture 14 relates periods to degree and singular homology.
