Lecture 25

Topological band theory

What this lecture does

Lecture 24 attached a line bundle to an isolated simple eigenvalue. For a periodic crystal, the parameters are momenta on a torus. The normalized curvature integral on that torus is an integer. For a two-band model we compute it as a degree, then use the cited Hall-response theorem to relate this integer to a measured conductance.

25.1 Bloch Hamiltonians and the momentum torus

Consider a square lattice with cell label r∈Z2r\in\Z^2r∈Z2 and NNN orbitals in each cell. Use lattice spacing one and a fixed orthonormal orbital basis. A one-particle wavefunction has a column ψr∈CN\psi_r\in\C^Nψr​∈CN at each cell. A translation-invariant finite-range Hamiltonian is given by (Hψ)r=∑R∈Z2TRψr+R,T−R=TR†,(H\psi)_r=\sum_{R\in\Z^2}T_R\psi_{r+R},\qquad T_{-R}=T_R^\dagger,(Hψ)r​=R∈Z2∑​TR​ψr+R​,T−R​=TR†​, where only finitely many hopping matrices TRT_RTR​ are nonzero. The adjoint condition makes the operator Hermitian. Recall the positive-phase Bloch convention from Quantum Mechanics I, Lecture 8. Substitution of ψr=eik⋅rv\psi_r=e^{\ii\mathbf k\cdot r}vψr​=eik⋅rv gives the Bloch Hamiltonian H(k)=∑RTReik⋅R.H(\mathbf k)=\sum_R T_R e^{\ii\mathbf k\cdot R}.H(k)=R∑​TR​eik⋅R. It is a smooth Hermitian N×NN\times NN×N matrix. Adding 2π2\pi2π to either momentum component leaves this matrix unchanged. Thus its parameter space is the Brillouin torus T2=R2/(2πZ)2\mathbb T^2=\R^2/(2\pi\Z)^2T2=R2/(2πZ)2, oriented by dkx∧dky\dd k_x\wedge\dd k_ydkx​∧dky​.

On an L×LL\times LL×L periodic lattice, the allowed momenta have components 2πa/L2\pi a/L2πa/L, with a∈{0,…,L−1}a\in\{0,\ldots,L-1\}a∈{0,…,L−1}. The finite geometric sum makes Bloch waves with distinct momenta orthogonal. There are L2L^2L2 such momenta, each paired with the NNN orbital columns. The resulting Bloch waves form a basis of the NL2NL^2NL2-dimensional one-particle space. The substitution above therefore gives its full block decomposition. The continuous torus describes the same matrix family as the system size grows. The infinite-lattice Bloch ansatz is not a normalized square-summable state.

A band is an energy branch En(k)E_n(\mathbf k)En​(k) of this family. We call a chosen smooth simple band isolated when, at every momentum, its distance from every other eigenvalue is at least a fixed δ>0\delta>0δ>0. This is a separation at the same momentum. It does not yet assert an energy interval separating occupied and empty states at all momenta.

Proposition 25.1 (The Bloch bundleproved). The eigenspaces of a smooth isolated simple band form a Hermitian line bundle Ln→T2L_n\to\mathbb T^2Ln​→T2. For local unit eigenvectors vi(k)v_i(\mathbf k)vi​(k), its connection is Ai=vi†dvi\Berryconn_i=v_i^\dagger\dd v_iAi​=vi†​dvi​. Its band Chern number is Cn=i2π∫T2dA.\bandChern_n=\frac\ii{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb T^2}\dd\Berryconn.Cn​=2πi​∫T2​dA.

Proof. The periodic matrix family is defined on the torus itself. Lecture 24’s adjugate construction supplies smooth local eigenvectors there. Their unit-phase transitions give the line bundle and the projected Hermitian connection. Its curvature is global and imaginary, so the displayed integral is real. The next proposition proves that it is an integer.

25.2 Integral flux and the two-band degree

A smooth family of Hermitian line bundles with connection here means one Hermitian line bundle with a smooth unitary connection on T2×[0,1]\mathbb T^2\times[0,1]T2×[0,1], restricted to the momentum slices.

Proposition 25.2 (Integrality on the torusproved). For a Hermitian line bundle with a unitary connection on the oriented torus, the number i∫T2F/(2π)\ii\int_{\mathbb T^2}F/(2\pi)i∫T2​F/(2π) is an integer. It is unchanged in a smooth family as defined above.

Proof. Pull the bundle and connection back along R2→T2\R^2\to\mathbb T^2R2→T2. Transport a fixed unit vector at the origin along radial segments. Lecture 21’s smooth parameter dependence gives a smooth unit frame on R2\R^2R2. Restrict it to the square [0,2π]2[0,2\pi]^2[0,2π]2 and call its connection form AAA. Write x=kxx=k_xx=kx​ and y=kyy=k_yy=ky​ in this argument. After identifying equal torus fibres, the edge frames satisfy e(2π,y)=e(0,y)hx(y),e(x,2π)=e(x,0)hy(x),e(2\pi,y)=e(0,y)h_x(y),\qquad e(x,2\pi)=e(x,0)h_y(x),e(2π,y)=e(0,y)hx​(y),e(x,2π)=e(x,0)hy​(x), with smooth maps hx,hy ⁣:[0,2π]→U(1)h_x,h_y\colon[0,2\pi]\to\U(1)hx​,hy​:[0,2π]→U(1). Lecture 3’s phase lifting on an interval gives real smooth functions χx\chi_xχx​ and χy\chi_yχy​ with hx=eiχxh_x=e^{\ii\chi_x}hx​=eiχx​ and hy=eiχyh_y=e^{\ii\chi_y}hy​=eiχy​. Smoothness follows locally by a smooth inverse of the exponential covering. The frame law gives AR−AL=idχxA_R-A_L=\ii\dd\chi_xAR​−AL​=idχx​ along the vertical edges and AT−AB=idχyA_T-A_B=\ii\dd\chi_yAT​−AB​=idχy​ along the horizontal edges. With Δχ=χ(2π)−χ(0)\Delta\chi=\chi(2\pi)-\chi(0)Δχ=χ(2π)−χ(0), Stokes therefore gives ∫T2F=i(Δχx−Δχy),i2π∫T2F=Δχy−Δχx2π.\int_{\mathbb T^2}F=\ii(\Delta\chi_x-\Delta\chi_y),\qquad \frac\ii{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb T^2}F =\frac{\Delta\chi_y-\Delta\chi_x}{2\pi}.∫T2​F=i(Δχx​−Δχy​),2πi​∫T2​F=2πΔχy​−Δχx​​. The right and top comparisons give the same frame at the last corner, so hy(0)hx(2π)=hx(0)hy(2π).h_y(0)h_x(2\pi)=h_x(0)h_y(2\pi).hy​(0)hx​(2π)=hx​(0)hy​(2π). Substitution of their lifted phases makes Δχx−Δχy\Delta\chi_x-\Delta\chi_yΔχx​−Δχy​ an integer multiple of 2π2\pi2π. This proves integrality. Neither edge phase is required to be a closed loop. In such a family, its global curvature restricts smoothly to the momentum slices, so the curvature integral varies continuously. An integer-valued continuous function on an interval is constant, proving the last claim. 

For a smooth Hamiltonian family whose chosen band stays isolated, the adjugate construction is smooth jointly in momentum and deformation parameter. The eigenline and projected connection of the chosen band give such a family.

For an example of integrality, the square potential A=−inxdy/(2π)A=-\ii n x\dd y/(2\pi)A=−inxdy/(2π), with n∈Zn\in\Zn∈Z, has hx(y)=e−inyh_x(y)=e^{-\ii n y}hx​(y)=e−iny and hy(x)=1h_y(x)=1hy​(x)=1. Its flux is −2πin-2\pi\ii n−2πin, so its Chern number is nnn. The edge phase at y=2πy=2\piy=2π agrees with its value at zero precisely because nnn is an integer.

Now take two orbitals per cell and a Hamiltonian H(k)=d(k)⋅σH(\mathbf k)=\mathbf d(\mathbf k)\cdot\boldsymbol\PauliH(k)=d(k)⋅σ, where d ⁣:T2→R3∖{0}\mathbf d\colon\mathbb T^2\to\R^3\setminus\{0\}d:T2→R3∖{0} is smooth. The Pauli product identity gives energies −∣d∣-|\mathbf d|−∣d∣ and +∣d∣+|\mathbf d|+∣d∣. Define the direction map d^=d/∣d∣\widehat{\mathbf d}=\mathbf d/|\mathbf d|d=d/∣d∣.

For the lower band and the outward orientation on S2\sphere2S2, dA=−i2d^⋅(∂kxd^×∂kyd^)dkx∧dky,C=deg⁡(d^).\dd\Berryconn=-\frac\ii2\widehat{\mathbf d}\cdot (\partial_{k_x}\widehat{\mathbf d}\times\partial_{k_y}\widehat{\mathbf d}) \dd k_x\wedge\dd k_y,\qquad \bandChern=\deg(\widehat{\mathbf d}).dA=−2i​d⋅(∂kx​​d×∂ky​​d)dkx​∧dky​,C=deg(d).

Thus it is a finite-range Hermitian lattice system.

25.4 Filled bands and the Hall response

At zero temperature, noninteracting electrons fill all states below the Fermi energy EFE_FEF​. An insulator has an open energy interval around EFE_FEF​ containing no bulk states. This full energy gap is stronger than separation of a band at each momentum. A Chern insulator is a band insulator whose occupied bands have nonzero total Chern number.

Let e>0e>0e>0 be the magnitude of the electron charge, so its signed charge is −e-e−e. Write Planck’s constant as hP=2πℏh_{\mathrm P}=2\pi\hbarhP​=2πℏ, distinct from a frame-change map. Fix the scalar Hall conductance by the transverse component convention Jy=σHEx,Jx=−σHEy.J_y=\Hallconductivity E_x,\qquad J_x=-\Hallconductivity E_y.Jy​=σH​Ex​,Jx​=−σH​Ey​.

Theorem 25.5 (TKNN responsecited). For a clean two-dimensional periodic noninteracting band insulator at zero temperature, with a bulk Fermi-energy gap and isolated occupied bands, the bulk linear Hall response is σH=e2hP∑n occupiedCn.\Hallconductivity=\frac{e^2}{h_{\mathrm P}}\sum_{n\ \text{occupied}}\bandChern_n.σH​=hP​e2​n occupied∑​Cn​. For one filled band the sum has one term.

This is the filled-band response theorem of Thouless, Kohmoto, Nightingale and den Nijs (TKNN, 1982). The Kubo-to-curvature step is cited, not proved here. In their p. 406 equation (5), the antisymmetrized derivative integral has the coefficient ie2/(2πhP)\ii e^2/(2\pi h_{\mathrm P})ie2/(2πhP​), matching the curvature normalization above. The response components in this lecture are fixed by the displayed definition.

The sign can also be checked in semiclassical notation. Let the real curvature be dAB=ΩBdkx∧dky\dd\Berryreal=\Omega_{\mathrm B}\dd k_x\wedge\dd k_ydAB​=ΩB​dkx​∧dky​. The wavepacket equations use ℏk˙=−eE\hbar\dot{\mathbf k}=-e\mathbf Eℏk˙=−eE and anomalous velocity −k˙×(ΩBez)-\dot{\mathbf k}\times(\Omega_{\mathrm B}\mathbf e_z)−k˙×(ΩB​ez​). Multiplication of this anomalous velocity by charge −e-e−e gives a current contribution proportional to (−Ey,Ex)(-E_y,E_x)(−Ey​,Ex​). For a filled band the group-velocity integral vanishes by periodicity. The momentum density of states is d2k/(2π)2\dd^2k/(2\pi)^2d2k/(2π)2, so the anomalous contribution gives J=e2ℏ∫T2d2k(2π)2ΩB(−Ey,Ex)=e2hPC(−Ey,Ex).\mathbf J=\frac{e^2}{\hbar}\int_{\mathbb T^2}\frac{\dd^2k}{(2\pi)^2} \Omega_{\mathrm B}(-E_y,E_x) =\frac{e^2}{h_{\mathrm P}}\bandChern(-E_y,E_x).J=ℏe2​∫T2​(2π)2d2k​ΩB​(−Ey​,Ex​)=hP​e2​C(−Ey​,Ex​). This checks the component sign and units of the cited formula. It is not a derivation of the Kubo theorem. For QWZ with only the lower band filled, the four insulating intervals have the conductances 000, +e2/hP+e^2/h_{\mathrm P}+e2/hP​, −e2/hP-e^2/h_{\mathrm P}−e2/hP​ and 000, in increasing uuu. Indeed, min⁡∣d∣>0\min|\mathbf d|>0min∣d∣>0 off the critical parameters, so EF=0E_F=0EF​=0 lies in the full gap between −min⁡∣d∣-\min|\mathbf d|−min∣d∣ and +min⁡∣d∣+\min|\mathbf d|+min∣d∣. At a critical parameter the gap hypothesis fails and the theorem does not assign that quantized value.

25.5 Gap closings and Dirac cones

A Dirac cone is a linear two-band crossing with local Hamiltonian HD(q)=vxqxσx+vyqyσy+mσz,vxvy≠0.H_{\mathrm D}(\mathbf q)=v_xq_x\Pauli_x+v_yq_y\Pauli_y+m\Pauli_z, \qquad v_xv_y\ne0.HD​(q)=vx​qx​σx​+vy​qy​σy​+mσz​,vx​vy​=0. Here q\mathbf qq measures displacement from the crossing momentum. The energies of this local Hamiltonian at m=0m=0m=0 are ±vx2qx2+vy2qy2\pm\sqrt{v_x^2q_x^2+v_y^2q_y^2}±vx2​qx2​+vy2​qy2​​. For QWZ the velocities at a vertical point are vx=cos⁡kxv_x=\cos k_xvx​=coskx​ and vy=cos⁡kyv_y=\cos k_yvy​=cosky​. Each local mass increases with uuu. The jumps are therefore +1+1+1 at −2-2−2, −1−1=−2-1-1=-2−1−1=−2 at zero and +1+1+1 at two, in agreement with the full degree proof.

Self-check.

Explain why the QWZ lower-band number is zero at u=3u=3u=3, without counting preimages.

Answer.

The component dz=3+cos⁡kx+cos⁡kyd_z=3+\cos k_x+\cos k_ydz​=3+coskx​+cosky​ is at least one. Thus the direction map lies in the northern hemisphere and contracts there to the north pole. Its degree is zero by Lecture 14’s homotopy invariance.

Summary

An isolated simple Bloch band defines a line bundle on the momentum torus. Its normalized curvature integral is an integer, fixed during any smooth deformation preserving band isolation. For H=d⋅σH=\mathbf d\cdot\boldsymbol\PauliH=d⋅σ with d≠0\mathbf d\ne0d=0, the lower-band number is C=deg⁡(d^)\bandChern=\deg(\widehat{\mathbf d})C=deg(d). For QWZ its values are 0,+1,−1,00,+1,-1,00,+1,−1,0 in increasing uuu, with undefined numbers at −2,0,2-2,0,2−2,0,2. The cited filled-band theorem gives the Hall conductance in units of e2/hPe^2/h_{\mathrm P}e2/hP​. The same principle of a homotopy class obstructing deformation will classify defects in Lecture 26.
