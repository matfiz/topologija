Lecture 25

Topological band theory

Lecture 24 attached a line bundle to an isolated simple eigenvalue. For a periodic crystal, the parameters are momenta on a torus. The normalized curvature integral on that torus is an integer. For a two-band model we compute it as a degree, then use the cited Hall-response law to relate this integer to a measured conductance.

Unit 25A Bloch bundles and the QWZ degree

Definition 25.3 (Band). A band is an energy branch En(k)E_n(\mathbf k)En​(k) of this family.

Physical input.

We model a slowly driven electron by a wavepacket, a superposition of Bloch states concentrated near a central momentum k\mathbf kk in an isolated band. Unit 25B Hall response and gap closings

25.4 Filled bands and the Hall response

We consider a thin flat sample in the xyxyxy plane, with contacts that allow a transverse current. A voltage applied along xxx produces a weak field ExE_xEx​. We measure the induced current per unit transverse length JyJ_yJy​. The bulk is the sample interior, away from its boundary. We call the periodic model clean when it has no disorder. Linear response is the term proportional to the applied weak field.

Definition 25.13 (Fermi energy). The Fermi energy EFE_FEF​ is the zero-temperature occupation threshold.

Physical input.

For noninteracting electrons at zero temperature, each one-particle state below EFE_FEF​ is occupied and each state above it is empty. Pauli exclusion permits one electron per state, with any spin already included among the orbitals.

Definition 25.14 (Band insulator). An insulator has an open energy interval around EFE_FEF​ containing no bulk states.

This full energy gap is stronger than separation of a band at each momentum.

Definition 25.15 (Chern insulator). A Chern insulator is a band insulator whose occupied bands have nonzero total Chern number.

For example, H=2cos⁡kxI+σzH=2\cos k_x I+\Pauli_zH=2coskx​I+σz​ has energies 2cos⁡kx−12\cos k_x-12coskx​−1 and 2cos⁡kx+12\cos k_x+12coskx​+1. Each band is isolated by a direct gap of two. Their energy ranges are [−3,1][-3,1][−3,1] and [−1,3][-1,3][−1,3], so no Fermi energy separates them completely. The Hamiltonian H=σzH=\Pauli_zH=σz​ instead has energies −1-1−1 and +1+1+1, with a full gap between them. The figure compares these periodic spectra, independent of kyk_yky​.

The electron has signed charge −e-e−e, as fixed in §25.1. Write Planck’s constant as hP=2πℏh_{\mathrm P}=2\pi\hbarhP​=2πℏ, distinct from a frame-change map.

We derive the anomalous velocity as a non-rigorous first-order adiabatic approximation. We use a packet narrow in momentum, a smooth isolated nondegenerate band and slow driving. We omit higher-order terms and sustained transitions between bands.

Self-check.

State in one sentence what the band Chern number is and why it was introduced. Explain why a nonzero value prevents a smooth periodic unit eigenvector.

Answer.

The band Chern number is the integer normalized curvature flux of an isolated eigenline over the momentum torus, introduced to classify gapped bands and determine their filled-band Hall response. A global periodic eigenvector would make A\BerryconnA global, so Stokes on the closed torus would force that flux to vanish.

Summary

An isolated simple Bloch band defines a line bundle on the momentum torus. Its normalized curvature integral is an integer, fixed during any smooth deformation preserving band isolation. For H=d⋅σH=\mathbf d\cdot\boldsymbol\PauliH=d⋅σ with d≠0\mathbf d\ne0d=0, the lower-band number is C=deg⁡(d^)\bandChern=\deg(\widehat{\mathbf d})C=deg(d). For QWZ its values are 0,+1,−1,00,+1,-1,00,+1,−1,0 in increasing uuu, with undefined numbers at −2,0,2-2,0,2−2,0,2. The cited filled-band response law gives the Hall conductance in units of e2/hPe^2/h_{\mathrm P}e2/hP​. The same principle of a homotopy class obstructing deformation will classify defects in Lecture 26.
