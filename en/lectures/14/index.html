Lecture 14

Degree, Poincaré–Hopf and de Rham’s theorem

What this lecture does

We prove that degree is an integer invariant under homotopy for smooth maps between nonempty closed connected oriented manifolds of the same positive dimension. Degree gives the smooth even-sphere hairy-ball and fixed-point theorems. Two cited theorems then relate vector-field zeros and periods to the homology of Part I.

14.1 Integration and top cohomology

Lecture 3 assigned a winding number to a loop in the circle. Give S1S^1S1 its counter-clockwise orientation and normalized angular form ν=αang/(2π)\nu=\alpha_{\mathrm{ang}}/(2\pi)ν=αang​/(2π) from Lecture 10. For each m∈Zm\in\Zm∈Z, the power map and its pullback are fm ⁣:S1⟶S1,fm(eiϕ)=eimϕ,fm∗ν=mν.f_m\colon S^1\longrightarrow S^1,\qquad f_m(e^{\ii\phi})=e^{\ii m\phi},\qquad f_m^*\nu=m\nu.fm​:S1⟶S1,fm​(eiϕ)=eimϕ,fm∗​ν=mν. Indeed, the angular lift mϕm\phimϕ has derivative mmm. The integral of the pullback is therefore mmm. Degree extends this oriented count to higher dimensions. Independence of the chosen integral-one form requires the following fact: a top form on a nonempty closed connected oriented manifold is exact if and only if its integral is zero.

All manifolds below are smooth. Cohomology has real coefficients. On an oriented nnn-manifold every nnn-form is closed, since all (n+1)(n+1)(n+1)-forms vanish. For a closed manifold, Stokes makes integration zero on exact forms. Integration therefore defines a linear map on Hn(M)\cohomology n(M)Hn(M).

Theorem 14.1 (Top cohomologysketched). For a nonempty closed connected oriented nnn-manifold MMM, IM ⁣:Hn(M)⟶R,IM[α]=∫MαI_M\colon\cohomology n(M)\longrightarrow\R, \qquad I_M[\alpha]=\int_M\alphaIM​:Hn(M)⟶R,IM​[α]=∫M​α is an isomorphism. Thus two top forms have equal integrals exactly when they differ by an exact form.

Sketch. For n=0n=0n=0, connected MMM is a point and integration is its signed value, an isomorphism. For n≥1n\ge1n≥1, a positive bump function in one positive chart gives a top form of positive integral. Rescaling it gives integral one, proving surjectivity. For injectivity the local input is this compact-support version of the Poincaré lemma: on Rn\R^nRn, a compactly supported top form of integral zero has a compactly supported primitive.

The local construction uses induction and the fundamental theorem of calculus. For n=1n=1n=1, integrate its coefficient from −∞-\infty−∞; zero total integral makes the primitive zero also beyond the support. For n>1n>1n>1, write the coefficient as f(x′,u)f(x',u)f(x′,u), set h(x′)=∫Rf(x′,u) duh(x')=\int_\R f(x',u)\,\dd uh(x′)=∫R​f(x′,u)du, and choose a compactly supported smooth function ρ(u)\rho(u)ρ(u) of integral one. Split the coefficient as (f−hρ)+hρ(f-h\rho)+h\rho(f−hρ)+hρ. The first term has zero integral in every uuu fibre, so integrating in uuu gives a compactly supported primitive, with the sign from moving du\dd udu past dx′\dd x'dx′. The second term has a primitive by the induction hypothesis applied to hdx′h\dd x'hdx′, whose integral is ∫f=0\int f=0∫f=0, and then wedged with ρdu\rho\dd uρdu.

On MMM, choose a finite cover by positive coordinate balls diffeomorphic to Rn\R^nRn. A partition of unity splits a top form into ball-supported pieces. Connectedness permits ordering the balls so that each after the first meets an earlier ball. In each chosen overlap take a top form of integral one. Starting with the last piece, subtract its integral times this overlap form and add the same form to the earlier piece. Repeating backwards makes every piece have integral zero, since the total is zero. The local construction now supplies primitives which extend by zero and sum to a global primitive. The cover and transfer details are the sketched steps. See Lee, Introduction to Smooth Manifolds, second edition, Theorems 17.30–17.31, pp. 454–455. 

For example, on outward S2S^2S2, the form β/(4π)\beta/(4\pi)β/(4π) from Lecture 13 has integral one. A 2-form α\alphaα of integral a∈Ra\in\Ra∈R consequently satisfies [α]=a[β/(4π)][\alpha]=a[\beta/(4\pi)][α]=a[β/(4π)].

14.2 The degree of a map

Definition 14.2 (Degree). Let MMM and NNN be nonempty closed connected oriented manifolds of the same dimension n≥1n\ge1n≥1. Let f ⁣:M→Nf\colon M\to Nf:M→N be smooth. Choose a top form ν\nuν on NNN with integral one. The degree of fff is deg⁡f=∫Mf∗ν.\mapdegree f=\int_M f^*\nu.degf=∫M​f∗ν.

Degree compares the orientations and will count inverse images with signs.

Theorem 14.3 (Degree and homotopyproved given top cohomology and cited smoothing). The degree is independent of ν\nuν. For every top form α\alphaα on NNN, ∫Mf∗α=(deg⁡f)∫Nα.\int_M f^*\alpha=(\mapdegree f)\int_N\alpha.∫M​f∗α=(degf)∫N​α. Homotopic smooth maps have the same degree. The identity has degree one, and a constant map has degree zero when n≥1n\ge1n≥1.

Proof. Two choices of ν\nuν differ by dη\dd\etadη, by the top-cohomology theorem. Their pullbacks differ by df∗η\dd f^*\etadf∗η, whose integral is zero by Stokes. For an arbitrary α\alphaα, write a=∫Nαa=\int_N\alphaa=∫N​α. The form α−aν\alpha-a\nuα−aν has integral zero, so it is exact. Pulling back and integrating gives the claimed formula.

For smoothly homotopic maps, the homotopy operator of Proposition 13.9 makes the pullbacks of ν\nuν differ by an exact form. Their integrals coincide. A continuous homotopy between smooth maps can be smoothed by Lee’s Theorem 6.29, recalled in Lecture 13, so the same conclusion holds. The identity pulls ν\nuν back to itself. A constant map has zero differential, hence pulls a positive-degree form back to zero. 

For example, the reflection (x,y)↦(x,−y)(x,y)\mapsto(x,-y)(x,y)↦(x,−y) on S1S^1S1 reverses the angular form, so its degree is −1-1−1. The degree also multiplies under composition. For a smooth map ggg from NNN to another such manifold of the same dimension, choose a top form ν\nuν of integral one on its target. Apply the integral formula to g∗νg^*\nug∗ν and then to ν\nuν to obtain deg⁡(g∘f)=(deg⁡g)(deg⁡f)\mapdegree(g\circ f)=(\mapdegree g)(\mapdegree f)deg(g∘f)=(degg)(degf).

Closedness matters. The map x↦x/2x\mapsto x/2x↦x/2 from [0,1][0,1][0,1] to itself pulls dx\dd xdx back to dx/2\dd x/2dx/2. The quotient of the two integrals is 1/21/21/2, but the interval has boundary, so this quotient is not a degree under our definition.

Self-check.

State in one sentence what degree measures and why the integral-one top form was introduced. Include the hypotheses on the source and target.

Answer.

For a smooth map between nonempty closed connected oriented manifolds of the same positive dimension, degree is the homotopy-invariant signed count of inverse images at a regular value expressed by integrating the pullback of a top form normalized to integral one. The decisive independence step is that two such forms differ by an exact form, whose pullback integrates to zero by Stokes.

14.7 Periods and singular homology

Recall from Lecture 6 that a singular cycle is a chain with zero boundary, and that Hk(M;R)\homology k(M;\R)Hk​(M;R) denotes homology with real coefficients. For a smooth singular simplex σ ⁣:Δk→M\sigma\colon\Delta^k\to Mσ:Δk→M, integration means ∫Δkσ∗α\int_{\Delta^k}\sigma^*\alpha∫Δk​σ∗α, extended linearly to chains. A smooth simplex here extends smoothly to a neighbourhood of its parameter simplex. Arbitrary continuous singular simplices cannot be integrated this way.

Theorem 14.14 (De Rhamcited). For every smooth manifold MMM and k≥0k\ge0k≥0, integration induces an isomorphism Hk(M)⟶Hom⁡Z(Hk(M;Z),R),[α]⟼([c]⟼∫cα).\cohomology k(M)\longrightarrow\Hom_\Z(\homology k(M;\Z),\R), \qquad [\alpha]\longmapsto\left([c]\longmapsto\int_c\alpha\right).Hk(M)⟶HomZ​(Hk​(M;Z),R),[α]⟼([c]⟼∫c​α). Here Hom⁡Z\Hom_\ZHomZ​ means homomorphisms of abelian groups. This includes replacing continuous cycles by smooth representatives. The cited construction uses Stokes on simplices, including their corners, and shows independence of all representatives. See Lee, Theorem 18.14, p. 484, with the comparison construction in Chapter 18.

A closed form with zero periods over all cycles is exact. Conversely, every homomorphism from integral homology to R\RR is the period functional of a closed form. Thus the theorem gives both uniqueness of a class from its periods and existence for every such assignment.

On the circle, a normalized angular period is both winding and degree. On the sphere, the normalized area period computes degree, while the sum of vector-field indices is the Euler characteristic, equal to two. On the torus, two independent angular periods give two classes in degree one. Its Euler characteristic is zero, consistent with a nonzero constant field descending from R2\R^2R2. The projective plane retains an order-two loop in integral homology, although every closed real 1-form has zero period around it.

Charts and tangent spaces made calculus possible on these spaces. Exterior differentiation and Stokes connected that calculus to cycles. The index of a zero and the degree of a map are integers obtained from this connection. Lecture 15 introduces a metric to measure lengths and define the Hodge star. These measurements add geometric information to the topological invariants above.

Summary

On a nonempty closed connected oriented manifold, top cohomology is measured by integration. Between such manifolds of equal positive dimension, degree is a homotopy invariant integer, computed by signed inverse images at a regular value. It gives the even-sphere hairy-ball theorem for smooth fields and the smooth Brouwer theorem. Poincaré–Hopf relates zero indices to the Euler characteristic. De Rham’s theorem identifies cohomology with real period functionals on homology. Part II adds a metric, connections and physical fields to these topological constructions.
