Lecture 18

Holonomy and Gauss–Bonnet

What this lecture does

Parallel transport around a loop can rotate a tangent vector. On a surface, the rotation is the enclosed curvature, modulo a full turn. Keeping track of the boundary tangent and its corner turns gives the Gauss–Bonnet formula. Summing that formula over a closed surface relates total curvature to its Euler characteristic.

18.1 Holonomy on an oriented surface

Let (M,g)(M,\metric)(M,g) be an oriented Riemannian surface with its Levi-Civita connection. Write dA\dd AdA for its metric area form. In a positive orthonormal frame e=(e1,e2)\tangentframe=(\tangentframe_1,\tangentframe_2)e=(e1​,e2​), recall Lecture 17’s convention ∇e1=−ω12e2,∇e2=ω12e1,dω12=KdA.\nabla\tangentframe_1=-\surfaceconn\tangentframe_2,\qquad \nabla\tangentframe_2=\surfaceconn\tangentframe_1,\qquad \dd\surfaceconn=\Gausscurv\dd A.∇e1​=−ω12​e2​,∇e2​=ω12​e1​,dω12​=KdA. The positive quarter-turn J\complexstructureJ is defined by Je1=e2\complexstructure\tangentframe_1=\tangentframe_2Je1​=e2​ and Je2=−e1\complexstructure\tangentframe_2=-\tangentframe_1Je2​=−e1​. It is independent of the positive orthonormal frame because plane rotations commute with this quarter-turn.

For a piecewise smooth loop γ\gammaγ based at ppp, its holonomy is the parallel-transport map Pγ ⁣:TpM→TpMP_\gamma\colon T_pM\to T_pMPγ​:Tp​M→Tp​M of Lecture 16, composed along its smooth segments. It is a rotation. To see the angle and its sign, write a transported unit vector along a framed segment as V=cos⁡α e1+sin⁡α e2,DVdt=(α˙−ω12(γ˙))JV.V=\cos\alpha\,\tangentframe_1+\sin\alpha\,\tangentframe_2,\qquad \frac{DV}{dt}=(\dot\alpha-\surfaceconn(\dot\gamma))\complexstructure V.V=cosαe1​+sinαe2​,dtDV​=(α˙−ω12​(γ˙​))JV. The derivative follows by the product rule and the two frame actions above. The angle α\alphaα has a real lift along the interval by Lecture 3’s circle lifting theorem. Parallel transport means α˙=ω12(γ˙)\dot\alpha=\surfaceconn(\dot\gamma)α˙=ω12​(γ˙​). Thus its matrix between the positive frames at the segment endpoints is the rotation through α(b)−α(a)\alpha(b)-\alpha(a)α(b)−α(a), measured counter-clockwise. The same construction works across finitely many curve segments. The figure illustrates the angle measured relative to a local frame.

Theorem 18.1 (Surface holonomyproved given partitions of unity). Let D⊂MD\subset MD⊂M be a compact disk with smooth boundary, contained in an open set with one positive orthonormal frame. Parallel transport around ∂D\partial D∂D, with its positive boundary orientation, rotates vectors by ΘD≡∫DKdA(mod2π).\Theta_D\equiv\int_D\Gausscurv\dd A\pmod{2\pi}.ΘD​≡∫D​KdA(mod2π).

Proof. The frame agrees with itself when the loop returns to its starting point. Integrating the parallel-angle equation and using Stokes gives α(b)−α(a)=∮∂Dω12=∫Ddω12=∫DKdA.\alpha(b)-\alpha(a)=\oint_{\partial D}\surfaceconn =\int_D\dd\surfaceconn=\int_D\Gausscurv\dd A.α(b)−α(a)=∮∂D​ω12​=∫D​dω12​=∫D​KdA. A tangent-plane rotation records this real angle only modulo 2π2\pi2π. Linearity of transport extends the unit-vector conclusion to every vector. 

Reversing the loop negates the angle. A different positive frame along the loop changes the net lifted angle α(b)−α(a)\alpha(b)-\alpha(a)α(b)−α(a) by an integer multiple of 2π2\pi2π. Indeed, its change-of-frame map is circle-valued, and its lifted angle has endpoint difference 2πm2\pi m2πm for some m∈Zm\in\Zm∈Z by Theorem 3.7. The rotation is independent of that choice. Stokes requires a frame on the whole disk, not merely near its boundary.

18.2 Geodesic triangles and angle excess

A geodesic triangle bounds an embedded disk by three regular geodesic segments, with nondegenerate corners. Its interior angles α1\alpha_1α1​, α2\alpha_2α2​ and α3\alpha_3α3​ lie in (0,2π)(0,2\pi)(0,2π). The signed exterior turn at a vertex is εi=π−αi∈(−π,π)\varepsilon_i=\pi-\alpha_i\in(-\pi,\pi)εi​=π−αi​∈(−π,π). It is the change from the incoming to the outgoing tangent, positive counter-clockwise in a positive tangent frame. The angle excess is α1+α2+α3−π\alpha_1+\alpha_2+\alpha_3-\piα1​+α2​+α3​−π. The spherical triangle in the figure has positive excess.

We use the cited turning-angle theorem: for the positive boundary of a disk in one positive frame extending over the disk, the continuous changes of tangent angle along the sides plus the exterior turns add to 2π2\pi2π. See Tu, Theorem 17.4, pp. 142–143. A frame defined only on an annulus does not satisfy this hypothesis. For a proof in a chart, see Lee, Theorem 9.1 and Lemma 9.2, pp. 158–163.

Theorem 18.2 (Triangle excessproved given turning and partitions of unity). If a geodesic triangle DDD lies in one framed neighbourhood as above, then α1+α2+α3−π=∫DKdA.\alpha_1+\alpha_2+\alpha_3-\pi=\int_D\Gausscurv\dd A.α1​+α2​+α3​−π=∫D​KdA.

Proof. Along each side, the unit tangent is parallel, so its frame angle changes by the integral of ω12\surfaceconnω12​ on that side. The turning theorem therefore gives ∮∂Dω12+∑i(π−αi)=2π.\oint_{\partial D}\surfaceconn+\sum_i(\pi-\alpha_i)=2\pi.∮∂D​ω12​+i∑​(π−αi​)=2π. Stokes gives ∮∂Dω12=∫DKdA\oint_{\partial D}\surfaceconn=\int_D\Gausscurv\dd A∮∂D​ω12​=∫D​KdA. Rearranging proves the formula. For this piecewise smooth boundary, Stokes follows by rounding each corner in a coordinate ball of radius ϵ\epsilonϵ. Smoothness bounds the changed connection integral by O(ϵ)O(\epsilon)O(ϵ) and the changed curvature integral by O(ϵ2)O(\epsilon^2)O(ϵ2). The replacement arcs have length O(ϵ)O(\epsilon)O(ϵ), including at concave corners. Apply smooth-boundary Stokes to the rounded disk and take ϵ→0\epsilon\to0ϵ→0. 

For example, the unit-sphere octant has curvature integral ∫0π/2∫0π/2sin⁡θ dθ dϕ=π2.\int_0^{\pi/2}\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin\theta\,\dd\theta\,\dd\phi=\frac{\pi}{2}.∫0π/2​∫0π/2​sinθdθdϕ=2π​. Its three right angles give the same excess 3π/2−π=π/23\pi/2-\pi=\pi/23π/2−π=π/2. This triangle is contained in a framed chart obtained by removing a point outside the octant from the sphere.

Its signed geodesic curvature is kg=g(∇TT,JT).\geodesiccurv=\metric(\nabla_{\tangentunit}\tangentunit, \complexstructure\tangentunit).kg​=g(∇T​T,JT). For a positive boundary, JT\complexstructure\tangentunitJT points into the disk. Let DDD be a compact embedded disk whose positive boundary has finitely many regular smooth sides and nondegenerate corners. Suppose one positive orthonormal frame is defined on a neighbourhood of all of DDD. Then ∫DKdA+∮∂Dkg ds+∑iεi=2π.\int_D\Gausscurv\dd A+\oint_{\partial D}\geodesiccurv\,\dd s +\sum_i\varepsilon_i=2\pi.∫D​KdA+∮∂D​kg​ds+i∑​εi​=2π. 18.4 Gauss–Bonnet on a closed surface

Theorem 18.4 (Gauss–Bonnetproved given cited topology and Stokes inputs). Every closed oriented Riemannian surface satisfies ∫MKdA=2πχ(M).\int_M\Gausscurv\dd A=2\pi\Eulerchar(M).∫M​KdA=2πχ(M).

Proof. Use the cited existence of a finite smooth triangulation of a compact surface (Lee, discussion before Theorem 9.7, pp. 166–167). In Lee’s definition, each curved triangle and its enclosed region lie in one coordinate chart, with regular sides and no cusps (p. 162). We also use the cited planar Schoenflies theorem: a simple closed plane curve and its enclosed region form a closed topological disk (Stillwell, §0.3.9, pp. 35–36). Gram–Schmidt on the connected chart neighbourhood of the face gives a positive orthonormal frame, changing one vector’s sign if needed. Thus each closed face is a disk to which the local formula applies. The edges need not be geodesics.

To obtain the required Δ\DeltaΔ-complex, order the vertices and choose one parametrization for each edge, from its lower to its higher vertex. These give a homeomorphism from the boundary of each standard triangle to its face boundary. Identify the standard triangle with a closed unit disk by radially rescaling rays from its centroid. Schoenflies supplies a disk homeomorphism; it can be chosen to match that boundary map. Indeed, any difference on the boundary circle is a homeomorphism ψ ⁣:S1→S1\psi\colon S^1\to S^1ψ:S1→S1, which extends to the disk by rζ↦rψ(ζ)r\zeta\mapsto r\psi(\zeta)rζ↦rψ(ζ), with the centre fixed. Composing with this extension matches the chosen boundary map. The face maps now agree on shared edges and give a finite Δ\DeltaΔ-complex structure on MMM.

Let VVV, EEE and FFF count vertices, edges and faces. Orient each face by the surface orientation and sum the local formula. Every shared edge has opposite boundary directions in its two faces. Reversing T\tangentunitT leaves ∇TT\nabla_{\tangentunit}\tangentunit∇T​T unchanged and negates JT\complexstructure\tangentunitJT, so it negates kg\geodesiccurvkg​. The two edge integrals cancel. At each vertex, the incident interior angles sum to 2π2\pi2π. Thus the sum of exterior turns over all face corners is 3πF−2πV3\pi F-2\pi V3πF−2πV. The figure displays these cancellations and the complete angle around a vertex.

The summed formula gives ∫MKdA=2πF−(3πF−2πV)=2πV−πF=2π(V−E+F).\int_M\Gausscurv\dd A =2\pi F-(3\pi F-2\pi V) =2\pi V-\pi F=2\pi(V-E+F).∫M​KdA=2πF−(3πF−2πV)=2πV−πF=2π(V−E+F). The last equality uses 3F=2E3F=2E3F=2E, since each triangular side belongs to two faces on a surface without boundary. By Theorems 6.2 and 6.3, V−E+F=χ(M)V-E+F=\Eulerchar(M)V−E+F=χ(M). For disconnected MMM, apply the argument to each component and add. 

The integral does not change when the metric changes, although the pointwise Gaussian curvature and the area form both change. A sphere therefore has total curvature 4π4\pi4π for every Riemannian metric. A torus has total curvature zero for every Riemannian metric, even when its curvature is not identically zero.

18.5 The Euler number of the tangent sphere

Recall from §14.5 that the index of an isolated tangent-field zero is the winding of its normalized components on a small positive circle. The cited Poincaré–Hopf theorem, Theorem 14.5, states that the sum of these indices on a closed oriented surface is its Euler characteristic. In particular this sum is independent of the chosen field with isolated zeros. For the tangent bundle of S2S^2S2, call this common sum its Euler number.

Corollary 18.5 (The tangent-sphere Euler numbersketched given Poincaré–Hopf). The Euler number of TS2TS^2TS2 is χ(S2)=2\Eulerchar(S^2)=2χ(S2)=2.

The field X(p)=e3−p3pX(p)=e_3-p_3pX(p)=e3​−p3​p from Lecture 14 has its two zeros at the poles, with index +1+1+1 at each. It supplies a field to which the definition applies. Poincaré–Hopf makes every such index sum equal to 222. A nowhere-zero tangent field would have empty index sum zero, contradicting this value. Hence there can be no global tangent frame on the sphere. A disk in one coordinate chart has a framed neighbourhood by Gram–Schmidt. The local curvature formula requires that the chosen frame extend over that disk, rather than only around its boundary; the whole sphere admits no such frame. Lecture 22 expresses the same integer using changes between local frames.

18.6 Latitude holonomy and the Foucault pendulum

The solid angle of a region on the unit sphere is its area. Let the latitude be λ∈(−π/2,π/2)\lambda\in(-\pi/2,\pi/2)λ∈(−π/2,π/2) and put a=π/2−λa=\pi/2-\lambdaa=π/2−λ. Follow its circle once in increasing ϕ\phiϕ. The figure shows the northern cap and unit vectors obtained from the parallel equation.

Example 18.6 (Latitude rotation). On the loop, the spherical frame has ω12=−cos⁡a dϕ\surfaceconn=-\cos a\,\dd\phiω12​=−cosadϕ. The spherical frame itself is undefined at the north pole, so Stokes cannot be applied to that frame across the cap.

Summary

Surface transport has angle ∫DKdA\int_D\Gausscurv\dd A∫D​KdA modulo 2π2\pi2π when one positive orthonormal frame covers the disk. The turning theorem adds the boundary tangent and corner turns, giving local Gauss–Bonnet. Using the cited topology inputs and summing over the triangulation yields ∫MKdA=2πχ(M)\int_M\Gausscurv\dd A=2\pi\Eulerchar(M)∫M​KdA=2πχ(M) on every closed oriented Riemannian surface. The tangent sphere has Euler number 222 by the cited Poincaré–Hopf theorem. Latitude transport gives the ideal Foucault angle −2πsin⁡λ-2\pi\sin\lambda−2πsinλ modulo 2π2\pi2π.
