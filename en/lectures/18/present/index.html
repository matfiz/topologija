Lecture 18

Holonomy and Gauss–Bonnet

Parallel transport around a loop can rotate a tangent vector. On a surface, the rotation is the enclosed curvature, modulo a full turn. Keeping track of the boundary tangent and its corner turns gives the Gauss–Bonnet formula. Summing that formula over a closed surface relates total curvature to its Euler characteristic. The ideal Foucault pendulum relates latitude holonomy to a slowly rotating swing line.

18.5 The Euler number of the tangent sphere

Recall from §14.5 that the index of an isolated tangent-field zero is the winding of its normalized components on a small positive circle. The cited Poincaré–Hopf theorem, Theorem 14.9, states that the sum of these indices on a closed oriented surface is its Euler characteristic. In particular this sum is independent of the chosen field with isolated zeros.

Definition 18.13. For the tangent bundle of S2S^2S2, call this common sum its Euler number.

Corollary 18.14 (The tangent-sphere Euler numbersketched given Poincaré–Hopf). The Euler number of TS2TS^2TS2 is χ(S2)=2\Eulerchar(S^2)=2χ(S2)=2.

Poincaré–Hopf makes every such index sum equal to 222. A nowhere-zero tangent field would have empty index sum zero, contradicting this value. Hence there can be no global tangent frame on the sphere. A disk in one coordinate chart has a framed neighbourhood by Gram–Schmidt. The local curvature formula requires that the chosen frame extend over that disk, rather than only around its boundary. The whole sphere admits no such frame. Self-check.

State in one sentence what holonomy is and why we introduced it. Explain why a holonomy rotation alone cannot recover the full curvature integral.

Answer.

Holonomy is parallel transport around a based loop as a map Pγ ⁣:TpM→TpMP_\gamma\colon T_pM\to T_pMPγ​:Tp​M→Tp​M, introduced to compare a vector with its return and measure curvature through the resulting rotation.
