Lecture 5

Van Kampen, surfaces and braids

What this lecture does

We compute loop classes in a space assembled from open pieces. Van Kampen’s theorem records how loops in the overlap become relations. For a surface presented by a polygon, its boundary gives the relation. For identical particles, the space consists of unordered configurations of distinct points. Loops in this space describe exchanges. Planar exchanges retain more information than their endpoint permutations, allowing arbitrary scalar phases.

5.1 Free groups and relations

A word in letters a1,…,aka_1,\ldots,a_ka1​,…,ak​ and their formal inverses is a finite string of these symbols. Delete adjacent inverse pairs until none remain. The resulting reduced word is unique. One algorithm reads from left to right, keeping a stack: cancel the last stored letter when the incoming letter is its inverse, and append it otherwise. Inserting an adjacent inverse pair leaves the final stack unchanged, even when the first inserted letter cancels a stored letter. The second then restores it. Thus reduction is independent of the order of deletions. A reduced word passes through the stack unchanged. Hence every complete sequence of deletions ends at the same stack output.

The free group FkF_kFk​ consists of reduced words. Multiply by concatenating and reducing. The empty word is the identity. Reverse a word and invert each letter to get its inverse. Reduction is unchanged by inserting or deleting inverse pairs in any context, so reducing after either stage of a triple concatenation gives the same result. This proves associativity. For example, ab−1ba−1b=b,aba−1b−1≠1in F2.ab^{-1}ba^{-1}b=b,\qquad aba^{-1}b^{-1}\ne1\quad\text{in }F_2.ab−1ba−1b=b,aba−1b−1=1in F2​. The second word is already reduced. A choice of images for the generators in any group extends uniquely to a homomorphism, by substituting in words. Inverse cancellations do not change the substituted group element.

A subgroup NNN of GGG is normal if gNg−1=NgNg^{-1}=NgNg−1=N for every g∈Gg\in Gg∈G. The quotient G/NG/NG/N identifies elements differing by an element of NNN. Its multiplication is inherited from GGG; normality makes this independent of representatives. The normal closure of specified words is the smallest normal subgroup containing them, equivalently the subgroup generated by all their conjugates and inverses. A presentation is written ⟨a1,…,ak∣r1,…,rs⟩=Fk/⟨ ⁣⟨r1,…,rs⟩ ⁣⟩.\langle a_1,\ldots,a_k\mid r_1,\ldots,r_s\rangle =F_k/\langle\!\langle r_1,\ldots,r_s\rangle\!\rangle.⟨a1​,…,ak​∣r1​,…,rs​⟩=Fk​/⟨⟨r1​,…,rs​⟩⟩. It imposes rj=1r_j=1rj​=1 in every word context, not merely deletion of one printed copy of a relator. For example, ⟨a∣am⟩≅Zm\langle a\mid a^m\rangle\cong\Z_m⟨a∣am⟩≅Zm​ for m≥1m\ge1m≥1, where Zm\Z_mZm​ is the group of integers modulo mmm. The free product G∗HG*HG∗H joins two groups without additional relations: words alternate nonidentity elements of the two groups, combining adjacent elements from the same group. In particular, ZZ=F2\Z*\Z=F_2ZZ=F2​.

5.3 Polygonal surfaces

A surface is a Hausdorff space with a countable basis in which every point has a neighbourhood homeomorphic to an open disc in R2\R^2R2. A compact surface with these local models is called closed. For g≥1g\ge1g≥1, let Σg\genussurface gΣg​ be the standard closed surface obtained from a 4g4g4g-gon by pairing equally labelled edges in the boundary word a1b1a1−1b1−1⋯agbgag−1bg−1.a_1b_1a_1^{-1}b_1^{-1}\cdots a_gb_ga_g^{-1}b_g^{-1}.a1​b1​a1−1​b1−1​⋯ag​bg​ag−1​bg−1​. The integer ggg is its genus. The paired arrows specify the same direction along each identified edge. Edge interiors join two half discs, and the cyclic corner identifications join to a disc. All vertices become one vertex, and the edges become a wedge of 2g2g2g circles. This is the polygonal model used in Hatcher, §1.2.

Proposition 5.2 (Surface groupsproved given van Kampen). For g≥1g\ge1g≥1, the fundamental group is π1(Σg)≅⟨a1,b1,…,ag,bg | ∏j=1g[aj,bj]⟩,[a,b]=aba−1b−1.\fundamentalgroup(\genussurface g) \cong\left\langle a_1,b_1,\ldots,a_g,b_g\ \middle|\ \prod_{j=1}^{g}[a_j,b_j]\right\rangle, \qquad [a,b]=aba^{-1}b^{-1}.π1​(Σg​)≅⟨a1​,b1​,…,ag​,bg​ ​ j=1∏g​[aj​,bj​]⟩,[a,b]=aba−1b−1.

Proof. Choose a point ooo in the face interior. Let U=Σg∖{o}U=\genussurface g\setminus\{o\}U=Σg​∖{o} and let VVV be a small open disc about ooo inside that face. The punctured polygon retracts radially to its boundary, fixing it. This deformation respects the edge identifications and retracts UUU onto the wedge of 2g2g2g circles. The set VVV is contractible. The overlap is a punctured disc, with fundamental group Z\ZZ. Its positive generator follows the face boundary once after radial retraction. Choose a path from its base point to the polygon vertex to read the word in the stated generators. The result is [a1,b1]⋯[ag,bg][a_1,b_1]\cdots[a_g,b_g][a1​,b1​]⋯[ag​,bg​]. Van Kampen kills exactly its normal closure, giving the presentation. A different connecting path conjugates the relator and leaves its normal closure unchanged. 

For g=1g=1g=1 the surface is the torus of Lecture 2; we compute its presentation explicitly in the final example.

5.6 The torus square

We compute the group of the square with opposite sides identified in the same direction. The quotient is the torus T2T^2T2 of Lecture 2. All four corners give one base point, and the two edge loops are aaa and bbb.

Removing the centre retracts the square quotient onto the two edge loops, so its group is F2F_2F2​. Adding the centre disc imposes the boundary relator by the open cover used in Proposition 5.2. Summary

Van Kampen combines path-connected open pieces with path-connected intersection by imposing the overlap relations. A polygonal surface has one relation, the product of its handle commutators. Planar configuration loops are braids; in three dimensions the ordered-space contraction reduces loop classes to permutations. The inverse outgoing permutation is the homomorphism in our path convention. Scalar exchange phases are arbitrary for braids and restricted to ±1\pm1±1 for permutations. Lecture 6 counts holes of every dimension by linear algebra.
