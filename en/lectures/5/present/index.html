Lecture 5

Van Kampen, surfaces and braids

We compute loop classes in a space assembled from open pieces. Van Kampen’s theorem records how loops in the overlap become relations. For a surface presented by a polygon, its boundary gives the relation. For identical particles, the space consists of unordered configurations of distinct points. Loops in this space describe exchanges. Planar exchanges retain more information than their endpoint permutations. Under a scalar statistical-transport assumption, these exchanges allow arbitrary unit phases.

Definition 5.2 (Word and reduced word). A word in letters a1,…,aka_1,\ldots,a_ka1​,…,ak​ and their formal inverses is a finite string of these symbols. A reduced word has no adjacent inverse pairs.

Delete adjacent inverse pairs until none remain. The resulting reduced word is unique. One algorithm reads from left to right, keeping a stack: cancel the last stored letter when the incoming letter is its inverse, and append it otherwise. Inserting an adjacent inverse pair leaves the final stack unchanged, even when the first inserted letter cancels a stored letter. The second then restores it. Thus reduction is independent of the order of deletions. A reduced word passes through the stack unchanged. Hence every complete sequence of deletions ends at the same stack output.

Definition 5.3 (Free group). The free group FkF_kFk​ consists of reduced words. Multiply by concatenating and reducing. The empty word is the identity. Reverse a word and invert each letter to get its inverse.

Coordinate permutations are homeomorphisms commuting with the projection, so they are deck transformations. They act transitively on a fibre and exhaust the deck group by uniqueness from one point. Physical input.

We consider a scalar statistical-transport sector for identical particles. The statistical factor multiplies a quantum amplitude, a complex number whose squared modulus gives a probability in the quantum description. Fix a base configuration x0∈Conf⁡n(Rd)x_0\in\Conf_n(\R^d)x0​∈Confn​(Rd). We assume that each collision-free loop based at x0x_0x0​ has a unit statistical factor. We assume that this factor depends only on endpoint-fixed homotopy, that successive loops multiply their factors, and that the constant loop has factor 111. This specifies the statistical part of transport after any phase depending on the detailed dynamics has been separated.

These assumptions define a homomorphism from the configuration-space fundamental group to the unit complex numbers. They do not follow from the return of the particle positions. A particular physical system must supply such a sector before the classification applies. For scalar factors, reversing composition order leaves the product unchanged.

Definition 5.19 (Unitary character and exchange phase). A one-dimensional unitary representation is a homomorphism χ ⁣:G→U(1)\chi\colon G\to U(1)χ:G→U(1), where U(1)={z∈C:∣z∣=1}U(1)=\{z\in\C:|z|=1\}U(1)={z∈C:∣z∣=1}. Summary

Van Kampen combines path-connected open pieces with path-connected intersection by imposing the overlap relations. A polygonal surface has one relation, the product of its handle commutators. Planar configuration loops are braids. In three dimensions the ordered-space contraction reduces loop classes to permutations. The inverse outgoing permutation is the homomorphism in our path convention. Under the scalar statistical-transport assumptions, exchange phases are arbitrary for braids and restricted to ±1\pm1±1 for permutations. Lecture 6 counts holes of every dimension by linear algebra.
