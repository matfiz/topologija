Lecture 7

Smooth manifolds

What this lecture does

A smooth manifold has local coordinates whose changes are smooth. This allows derivatives to be compared between coordinate systems. We construct coordinates on spheres and projective spaces, then prove that regular constraints define manifolds using the implicit function theorem. The space of pure qubit states gives a concrete example: a sphere described by two complex coordinate charts.

7.1 Charts and smooth structures

A topological nnn-manifold is a Hausdorff, second-countable space MMM locally homeomorphic to open subsets of Rn\R^nRn. Here second-countable means that its topology has a countable basis. A chart is a pair (U,φ)(U,\varphi)(U,φ), with U⊆MU\subseteq MU⊆M open and φ ⁣:U→φ(U)⊆Rn\varphi\colon U\to\varphi(U)\subseteq\R^nφ:U→φ(U)⊆Rn a homeomorphism onto an open subset. We consider manifolds without boundary. The component functions of the chart are its coordinates, written x1,…,xnx^1,\ldots,x^nx1,…,xn. The superscripts are coordinate labels, not powers.

Two charts are smoothly compatible when both coordinate changes on their overlap are smooth. For charts (U,φ)(U,\varphi)(U,φ) and (V,ψ)(V,\psi)(V,ψ), one such change is ψ∘φ−1 ⁣:φ(U∩V)⟶ψ(U∩V).\psi\circ\varphi^{-1}\colon\varphi(U\cap V)\longrightarrow\psi(U\cap V).ψ∘φ−1:φ(U∩V)⟶ψ(U∩V). Smooth means C∞C^\inftyC∞: all orders of partial derivatives exist and are continuous. An empty overlap imposes no condition. A smooth atlas is a collection of pairwise compatible charts covering MMM. A smooth structure is a maximal smooth atlas, containing every chart compatible with it. A smooth manifold is a topological manifold with a smooth structure.

Any smooth atlas determines a unique smooth structure. To prove this, adjoin every chart compatible with the given atlas. For two added charts ψ\psiψ and θ\thetaθ and a point in their overlap, choose an original chart φ\varphiφ containing that point. Locally the transition is θ∘ψ−1=(θ∘φ−1)∘(φ∘ψ−1).\theta\circ\psi^{-1} =(\theta\circ\varphi^{-1})\circ(\varphi\circ\psi^{-1}).θ∘ψ−1=(θ∘φ−1)∘(φ∘ψ−1). Both factors are smooth. Reversing the roles gives a smooth inverse. Thus the enlarged collection is an atlas. Any chart compatible with the enlarged collection was compatible with the original one and has already been added. This proves maximality. Every chart of any maximal atlas containing the original atlas belongs to this enlarged collection. That maximal atlas must therefore equal the enlarged collection, proving uniqueness.

The smooth structure is extra data. On the topological line, the global charts x(t)=tx(t)=tx(t)=t and y(t)=t3y(t)=t^3y(t)=t3 individually define smooth structures. Their transition y∘x−1(s)=s3y\circ x^{-1}(s)=s^3y∘x−1(s)=s3 is smooth, but its inverse is not differentiable at zero. Along s=1/k3s=1/k^3s=1/k3, its difference quotient is (1/k)/(1/k3)=k2(1/k)/(1/k^3)=k^2(1/k)/(1/k3)=k2, which is unbounded as k→∞k\to\inftyk→∞. Hence the two charts do not belong to one smooth atlas. The resulting manifolds will nevertheless be diffeomorphic, by a map different from the identity.

7.3 Smooth maps and the chain rule

Let MMM and NNN be smooth manifolds of dimensions nnn and mmm. A continuous map f ⁣:M→Nf\colon M\to Nf:M→N is smooth if, near every p∈Mp\in Mp∈M, its coordinate expression is smooth. For charts φ\varphiφ near ppp and ψ\psiψ near f(p)f(p)f(p), this expression is f^=ψ∘f∘φ−1 ⁣:φ(U∩f−1(V))⟶ψ(V).\widehat f=\psi\circ f\circ\varphi^{-1} \colon\varphi(U\cap f^{-1}(V))\longrightarrow\psi(V).f​=ψ∘f∘φ−1:φ(U∩f−1(V))⟶ψ(V). 7.5 The qubit’s projective state space

A pure qubit state is a nonzero column (a,b)∈C2(a,b)\in\C^2(a,b)∈C2, with two columns representing the same state when they differ by a nonzero complex scalar. Summary

A smooth structure is specified by charts with smooth transitions. Smooth maps are tested in these charts, and the Euclidean chain rule makes the test independent of coordinates. The implicit function theorem turns independent smooth constraints into embedded submanifolds. The qubit state space has the two projective charts zzz and w=1/zw=1/zw=1/z, and is diffeomorphic to the Bloch sphere.
