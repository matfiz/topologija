Sources cited in the lecture notes.

Reading by lecture

The same references and locators as in each lecture PDF.

Lecture 1 · Spaces and continuity

Munkres, Topology, second edition, §§12–13 and 15–18: topologies, bases, products, subspaces, separation and continuity.

Lecture 2 · Compactness, connectedness and gluing

Munkres, Topology, second edition, §§22–28; Heine–Borel is Theorem 27.3, metric sequential compactness is Theorem 28.2, and the sine curve is §24, Example 7.

Lecture 3 · Homotopy and the fundamental group

Hatcher, Algebraic Topology, §1.1, pp. 25–38 (Theorem 1.8 and Proposition 1.18); Munkres, Topology, §§51–56, §58 and Lemma 27.5.

Lecture 4 · Covering spaces and rotations

Hatcher, Algebraic Topology, Proposition 1.14, §1.3, especially Propositions 1.30–1.34 and Theorem 1.38; Munkres, Topology, §§53–54. Nakahara, Geometry, Topology and Physics, §4.6.5 and Table 4.1.

Lecture 5 · Van Kampen, surfaces and braids

Hatcher, Algebraic Topology, §1.2; Nakahara, Geometry, Topology and Physics, §4.4; Simon, Topological Quantum, §§3.3–3.5 and Exercise 3.1.

Lecture 6 · Homology and the Euler characteristic

Hatcher, Algebraic Topology, §2.1, pp. 102–107, Theorems 2.27 and 2A.1, and §2.2, pp. 146–147; Nakahara, Geometry, Topology and Physics, §3.3; Kosniowski, A First Course in Algebraic Topology, Theorem 11.3 and Chapter 29; Kitaev, Fault-tolerant quantum computation by anyons (2003); section numbers here refer to arXiv:quant-ph/9707021v1, §1.

Lecture 7 · Smooth manifolds

Tu, An Introduction to Manifolds, §§5–7, Theorem 9.9 and §11; the irrational-line example is Example 15.9. Nakahara, Geometry, Topology and Physics, §5.1.

Lecture 8 · Tangent spaces, Lie groups and homogeneous spaces

Tu, An Introduction to Manifolds, §§8, 12, 15–16; Lee, Introduction to Smooth Manifolds, second edition, Theorems 20.12, 21.17 and 21.18.

Lecture 9 · Vector fields and flows

Tu, An Introduction to Manifolds, §14 and §20.2; Lee, Introduction to Smooth Manifolds, second edition, Theorems 9.12 and 19.12.

Lecture 10 · Differential forms

Tu, An Introduction to Manifolds, §§17–19; Linear Algebra, Lecture 12, exterior powers and the determinant wedge.

Lecture 11 · Cartan’s calculus

Tu, An Introduction to Manifolds, §20.3–§20.5, especially Theorem 20.10, pp. 229–230.

Lecture 12 · Orientation, integration and Stokes’ theorem

Tu, An Introduction to Manifolds, §13 and Appendix C; §§21–23, especially Theorem 23.12, pp. 269–271.

Lecture 13 · De Rham cohomology

Tu, An Introduction to Manifolds, §24–§29, especially §26, pp. 288–291, and §29, pp. 311–317. Lee, Introduction to Smooth Manifolds, second edition, Theorems 6.26 and 6.29, pp. 141–143.

Lecture 14 · Degree, Poincaré–Hopf and de Rham’s theorem

Lee, Introduction to Smooth Manifolds, second edition, Theorems 17.30–17.31, pp. 454–455; Theorem 17.35, pp. 457–458; Theorems 17.38–17.39, pp. 459–460; Theorem 18.14, p. 484. Milnor, Topology from the Differentiable Viewpoint, §6, pp. 32–35 (online scan).

Lecture 15 · Metrics, the Hodge star and Maxwell’s equations

Nakahara, Geometry, Topology and Physics, second edition, §7.1 and §7.9, especially (7.172)–(7.177), Example 7.16, and Theorems 7.7–7.8. Tu, Differential Geometry, Theorem 1.12, pp. 6–7, and §16.4–§16.5, pp. 132–135.

Lecture 16 · Connections and geodesics

Tu, Differential Geometry, §6, §13–§15, especially Problems 15.4–15.5, and Theorem 16.6, p. 131. Lee, Riemannian Manifolds: An Introduction to Curvature, Theorem 5.4, pp. 68–70, and Lemma 6.2, pp. 94–95. Nakahara, §7.2, §7.4 and §7.7.

Lecture 17 · Curvature

Tu, Differential Geometry, §10–§12, especially Theorems 11.1 and 11.7 and Theorem 12.3, p. 90. Lee, Riemannian Manifolds: An Introduction to Curvature, Chapter 7, Propositions 7.1, 7.4 and 7.5. Nakahara, §7.3 and §7.8.

Lecture 18 · Holonomy and Gauss–Bonnet

Tu, Differential Geometry, §17, especially Theorems 17.4–17.5 and 17.9, pp. 142–146. Lee, Riemannian Manifolds: An Introduction to Curvature, Ch. 9, pp. 158–168, including the smooth triangulation discussion. Stillwell, Classical Topology and Combinatorial Group Theory, §0.3.9, pp. 35–36 (Schoenflies theorem).

Lecture 19 · Lorentzian geometry and general relativity

Nakahara, §7.10.1–2 and Example 7.15. Carroll, Lecture Notes on General Relativity (1997), §4, especially pp. 104–116, and §7, pp. 172–185.

Lecture 20 · Fibre bundles

Nakahara, §9.2–9.4, especially Theorem 9.2. Naber, §§4.2–4.4, especially Theorem 4.3.4, pp. 223–224 (continuous principal reconstruction), and Theorem 4.4.3, pp. 231–232 (topological clutching). Smooth reconstruction follows Nakahara, §9.2.2, with the row-frame convention used here. The homogeneous-bundle proof uses Lee, Introduction to Smooth Manifolds, second edition, Theorem 4.5, for submersion coordinates. The associated-bundle section correspondence also appears in Lie Groups, Lecture 26, From equivariant functions to sections.

Lecture 21 · Gauge fields: connections, curvature and holonomy

Nakahara, §10.1.3–4, §10.3.4–5, §10.4.2 and §10.5.1, 10.5.3.

Lecture 22 · Chern classes and the Dirac monopole

Nakahara, §10.5.2 and §11.1–11.2. Tu, Differential Geometry, §25.8, p. 235. Gunning, Theorem 2.2, (2.3) and Corollary 2.3, pp. 34–35, for the holomorphic classification.

Lecture 23 · Yang–Mills theory and instantons

Tu, Differential Geometry, §28.1, §29.4 and §31.3–31.5. Nakahara, §10.5.4–10.5.5, §11.3.1 (11.54)–(11.55), §11.5 and §12.6 (12.90). Tong, Gauge Theory, §2.1–2.3, for the action, topological phase and semiclassical Euclidean input.
